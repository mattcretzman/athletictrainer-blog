# Fort Carson Athletic Trainer Jobs: Your Complete Colorado Springs Guide

Fort Carson sits at the base of the Rocky Mountains just south of Colorado Springs, and it has become one of the most sought-after duty stations for certified athletic trainers entering the military healthcare space. Between the Army's accelerating investment in the Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program and the installation's sheer size — approximately 24,000 active-duty soldiers — the demand for qualified athletic trainers here continues to grow.

If you are weighing a career move to this Colorado installation, this guide covers everything you need to make a confident decision: position types, salary expectations, qualification requirements, the Colorado Springs lifestyle, and a clear path to applying.

## Why Fort Carson Is a Top Destination for Athletic Trainer Jobs

Fort Carson, located in Colorado Springs, CO, offers certified athletic trainers multiple employment pathways including government contractor roles, GS civil service positions, and H2F (Holistic Health and Fitness) performance team jobs supporting approximately 24,000 active-duty soldiers. These positions offer competitive salary packages that vary by installation location and contract terms.

### Fort Carson Overview: Size, Mission, and Troop Population

Fort Carson is one of the largest Army installations in the United States, spanning more than 137,000 acres. Known as "The Mountain Post," the installation serves as a major force projection platform, meaning its units regularly deploy around the world. That operational tempo places tremendous physical demands on service members — and creates a sustained need for athletic trainers who can help keep them mission-ready.

The installation's infrastructure includes robust fitness and medical facilities such as the Iron Horse Sports and Fitness Center, Ivy Physical Fitness Center, and multiple brigade aid stations and clinics. For an athletic trainer, this translates to well-equipped work environments with access to treatment modalities, rehabilitation spaces, and field training areas.

### Key Units and Brigades That Employ Athletic Trainers

Fort Carson hosts several major commands that drive athletic trainer hiring:

- **4th Infantry Division** — The installation's flagship unit, comprising multiple Brigade Combat Teams (BCTs) that are primary targets for H2F staffing.
- **10th Special Forces Group (Airborne)** — Special operations forces with particularly high demand for tactical human performance support.
- **4th Combat Aviation Brigade** — Aviation units with unique physical demands and injury profiles.
- **71st Ordnance Group (Explosive Ordnance Disposal)** — Specialized teams requiring targeted musculoskeletal care.

Each of these units presents a distinct clinical population. An athletic trainer working with the 10th Special Forces Group, for example, encounters different injury patterns and performance demands than one embedded with a conventional infantry brigade.

### The Growth of H2F and Tactical Performance Roles at Fort Carson

The Army's Holistic Health and Fitness system represents the service's primary investment in soldier readiness and lethality. Fort Carson was designated as one of the Army's initial H2F implementation sites, positioning it as a hub for tactical human performance positions.

Each H2F performance team typically includes one to two certified athletic trainers per battalion-level unit. With full H2F implementation across all Brigade Combat Teams expected by 2030, Fort Carson alone could sustain dozens of athletic trainer positions at any given time. This is not speculative growth — it is a funded, directed Army program with documented hiring timelines.

## Types of Athletic Trainer Positions at Fort Carson

Understanding the distinctions between position types is essential for aligning your career goals with the right opportunity.

### Government Contractor Athletic Trainer Roles

The majority of athletic trainer positions at Fort Carson are filled through government contractors like PSI. In this model, you are employed by the contracting company rather than the federal government. The contractor holds a contract with the Department of Defense to provide healthcare personnel at the installation.

Contractor roles tend to offer faster hiring timelines compared to federal positions and often include competitive benefits packages. Contract lengths typically range from one to five years, with option years built in for extensions. Your day-to-day duties are defined by the contract's Performance Work Statement, which outlines the scope of care you provide.

### GS (Civil Service) Athletic Trainer Positions

When available, federal civil service positions for athletic trainers at Fort Carson generally fall under GS-9 to GS-11 pay grades. These roles come with the full suite of federal employee benefits, including the Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS), Thrift Savings Plan (TSP), and federal health insurance.

GS positions are posted on USAJobs.gov and follow a structured federal hiring process that can take several months. They appear less frequently than contractor roles but offer long-term job stability and a defined career ladder within the Department of Defense.

### H2F Performance Team Athletic Trainer Jobs

H2F positions represent the fastest-growing category of athletic trainer jobs at Fort Carson. As a member of an H2F performance team, you work alongside strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, dietitians, occupational therapists, and cognitive performance specialists.

Your role on the H2F team centers on injury prevention, movement screening, acute injury assessment, rehabilitation support, and return-to-duty protocols. You are embedded directly with the unit you support, which means you spend significant time in the field — at ranges, during physical training, and on ruck marches — rather than solely in a clinic setting.

### Sports Medicine Clinic and Rehabilitation Roles

Fort Carson's Evans Army Community Hospital and its associated clinics also employ athletic trainers in more traditional sports medicine and rehabilitation settings. These positions may focus on post-surgical rehabilitation, chronic injury management, and supporting the broader beneficiary population including military families.

## Qualifications and Requirements for Fort Carson AT Jobs

Whether you pursue a contractor or federal position, the baseline requirements for working as an athletic trainer at Fort Carson are consistent.

### BOC Certification and Colorado State Licensure (DORA)

You must hold current Board of Certification (BOC) certification. In addition, Colorado requires athletic trainers to obtain a state license through the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA). The DORA licensure process involves verifying your BOC certification, submitting an application, and paying applicable fees. Plan to initiate this process early, as you will need an active Colorado license before your start date.

### Security Clearance and Background Check Requirements

All personnel working on Fort Carson must pass a background investigation. For most athletic trainer roles, this involves a National Agency Check with Inquiries (NACI) or a Tier 1 investigation. Some positions — particularly those supporting special operations units like the 10th Special Forces Group — may require a higher-level security clearance.

The background check process typically takes four to twelve weeks but can extend longer depending on your history and the clearance level required. You should be prepared to provide a detailed employment history, residential addresses, and references.

### Preferred Experience: Tactical, Orthopedic, and Military Populations

While not always mandatory, the following experience areas strengthen your candidacy:

- **Prior military or tactical population experience** — Working with service members, law enforcement, or firefighters.
- **Orthopedic rehabilitation** — Post-surgical protocols, manual therapy, and functional movement assessment.
- **Emergency and field care** — Comfort providing care in austere or non-clinical environments.
- **Interdisciplinary teamwork** — Experience collaborating with physical therapists, strength coaches, and physicians.

<Callout type="tip">
If you are a recent graduate without military-specific experience, highlight any clinical rotations involving orthopedic populations, emergency settings, or high-intensity athletic programs. The core clinical skills transfer directly to tactical environments.
</Callout>

### Physical Requirements and Altitude Considerations

Fort Carson sits at approximately 6,000 feet of elevation. If you are relocating from sea level, expect an adjustment period. Altitude affects both your personal physiology and your clinical practice — dehydration risk, acclimatization protocols, and exercise intensity guidelines all shift at elevation.

Many positions also require the ability to traverse uneven terrain, carry equipment into field locations, and work outdoors in variable weather conditions ranging from high-altitude sun exposure to Colorado's winter storms.

## Salary and Benefits for Athletic Trainers at Fort Carson

Compensation varies based on whether you hold a contractor or federal position, your experience level, and the specific employer.

### Contractor Salary Ranges and Benefits Packages

Athletic trainer positions at Fort Carson offer competitive salary packages that vary by installation location and contract terms. Positions supporting special operations units may command enhanced compensation.

Benefits packages from contractors like PSI generally include:

- Health, dental, and vision insurance
- Paid time off and federal holiday observance
- 401(k) or retirement plan options
- Continuing education support
- Relocation assistance (varies by contract)

### GS Pay Scale and Locality Adjustments for Colorado Springs

GS-9 to GS-11 positions include a locality pay adjustment for the Colorado Springs area. GS pay scales are publicly available and include locality adjustments. Higher step levels, earned through time in service and performance, increase compensation over time.

Federal positions also include benefits that carry substantial value beyond base salary: FERS retirement, TSP with employer matching up to 5%, comprehensive health insurance, and generous leave accrual.

### Comparing Military AT Compensation to Civilian Roles in Colorado

Military contractor and federal positions at Fort Carson offer competitive compensation compared to civilian athletic training roles in the Colorado Springs market, with the added benefit of working in a mission-driven environment alongside a multidisciplinary team.

When evaluating total compensation, factor in that many contractor positions include federal holidays off (typically 10-11 per year) in addition to paid leave, which can exceed what civilian employers offer.

## Living in Colorado Springs: A Guide for Relocating Athletic Trainers

Colorado Springs consistently ranks among the most desirable mid-size cities in the United States for quality of life, and for good reason.

### Cost of Living Breakdown: Housing, Transportation, and Daily Expenses

Colorado Springs has a cost of living approximately 5-8% above the national average. The primary driver is housing: median home prices hover around $450,000 as of 2024, and average rent for a two-bedroom apartment ranges from $1,400 to $1,800 depending on the neighborhood.

Other costs — groceries, transportation, healthcare — align closely with national averages. Colorado has no state sales tax on groceries, which provides a modest offset. The state income tax rate is a flat 4.4%.

### Best Neighborhoods Near Fort Carson for Commuters

The following areas offer reasonable commute times to Fort Carson's main gates:

- **Fountain** — The closest town to Fort Carson's Gate 20, with affordable housing and a growing community.
- **Security-Widefield** — Adjacent to the installation with quick access and family-friendly neighborhoods.
- **South Colorado Springs (Broadmoor area)** — Slightly higher cost but offers access to both the installation and downtown amenities.
- **Pueblo West** — A more affordable option approximately 30 minutes south, suitable if you prioritize housing cost savings.

### Outdoor Lifestyle and Recreation Opportunities

Colorado Springs offers world-class outdoor recreation that directly appeals to the athletic trainer population. Garden of the Gods, Pikes Peak, Cheyenne Mountain State Park (immediately adjacent to Fort Carson), and hundreds of miles of trails provide year-round opportunities for hiking, mountain biking, trail running, rock climbing, and skiing within 90 minutes.

The city's culture is health-conscious and active, which means you will find a professional and social community aligned with your interests in human performance.

### Colorado Springs Healthcare and Professional Community

Colorado Springs has a robust healthcare ecosystem. UCHealth, Centura Health, and the U.S. Olympic and Paralympic Training Center all maintain a significant presence in the area. This creates networking opportunities, continuing education access, and potential secondary employment avenues if your contract or position allows.

The Colorado Athletic Trainers' Association (CoATA) hosts regular events and provides advocacy support for licensure and scope-of-practice issues relevant to your career.

## How to Apply for Athletic Trainer Jobs at Fort Carson

The application process differs depending on whether you are pursuing a contractor or federal role.

### Applying Through PSI and Other Military Healthcare Contractors

PSI regularly posts athletic trainer openings at Fort Carson and other military installations. The contractor application process is generally more streamlined than the federal route:

1. **Submit your application** through the contractor's career portal or job board listing.
2. **Complete initial screening** — a recruiter reviews your credentials, licensure, and experience.
3. **Interview** with the hiring manager or program director.
4. **Credentialing and background check** — this phase runs concurrently with onboarding paperwork.
5. **Start date confirmation** — once your background check clears and credentialing is complete.

<Callout type="tip">
When applying through PSI, ensure your resume explicitly lists your BOC certification number, Colorado DORA license status (or application status), and any experience with military or tactical populations. Hiring managers screen for these qualifications first.
</Callout>

### Navigating USAJobs for Federal AT Positions

Federal GS positions are listed exclusively on [USAJobs.gov](https://www.usajobs.gov). Search using job series 0601 (General Health Science) or keywords such as "athletic trainer" and "Fort Carson." Federal applications require a detailed resume formatted to USAJobs standards, which is significantly more comprehensive than a standard civilian resume.

Key tips for USAJobs applications:

- Include every relevant qualification, certification, and experience — federal HR specialists assess eligibility based on what is explicitly stated.
- Address every requirement listed in the job announcement.
- Prepare for a timeline of 60 to 120 days from application to offer.

### Tips for Tailoring Your Resume for Military AT Roles

Your resume should translate clinical skills into language that resonates with military healthcare hiring managers:

- Replace "athletes" with "tactical populations" or "physically demanding occupations" where applicable.
- Emphasize injury prevention and return-to-duty outcomes rather than solely treatment metrics.
- Highlight any experience with interdisciplinary teams, which mirrors the H2F model.
- Quantify your impact: number of patients managed, injury reduction rates, or program development contributions.

### What to Expect During the Hiring and Onboarding Process

From application to your first day at Fort Carson, expect the full process to take six to twelve weeks for contractor roles and potentially longer for federal positions. During onboarding, you will complete installation access paperwork, receive a Common Access Card (CAC), participate in orientation briefings, and integrate with your assigned unit or team.

## A Day in the Life of a Fort Carson Athletic Trainer

Understanding the daily rhythm of this role helps you assess whether it matches your professional goals.

### Typical Daily Schedule and Responsibilities

A typical day for an H2F-embedded athletic trainer at Fort Carson might look like this:

- **0600-0730** — Attend morning physical training with the unit, providing real-time injury assessment and movement observation.
- **0730-0830** — Conduct sick call triage for musculoskeletal complaints, determining which service members need same-day treatment versus referral.
- **0830-1130** — Provide individual treatment sessions: manual therapy, therapeutic exercise, taping, and modality application.
- **1130-1230** — Lunch and documentation.
- **1230-1500** — Movement screens, injury prevention education sessions, or field coverage for training events.
- **1500-1630** — Follow-up appointments, team meetings with H2F staff, and administrative tasks.

### Working with Soldiers vs. Traditional Athletes

Service members present a unique clinical population. Unlike traditional athletes who have an offseason, soldiers maintain year-round physical demands with operational deployments, field exercises, and mandatory fitness testing. Injuries often result from cumulative overuse — rucking, repetitive load carriage, and high-volume running — rather than acute sporting events.

You also navigate a military chain of command, which influences treatment compliance, return-to-duty decisions, and communication pathways. Building trust with unit leadership is as important as building rapport with individual patients.

### Collaboration with Multidisciplinary H2F Teams

The H2F model is explicitly interdisciplinary. You will collaborate daily with strength and conditioning coaches on training load management, coordinate with physical therapists on rehabilitation progressions, consult with dietitians on recovery nutrition, and work with cognitive performance specialists on stress management strategies that affect injury risk.

This team-based approach is one of the most professionally rewarding aspects of military athletic training. It expands your clinical perspective and creates opportunities for cross-disciplinary skill development.

### Career Growth and Advancement Opportunities

Fort Carson's size and H2F investment create a natural career ladder. Athletic trainers can advance to senior clinician roles, team lead positions, or program coordinator roles overseeing athletic training services across multiple units. Some athletic trainers transition into broader human performance management or pursue additional credentials in areas like tactical strength and conditioning (TSAC-F) or performance enhancement specialization.

## Fort Carson vs. Other Colorado Military Installations for AT Jobs

Colorado hosts several military installations, but not all offer equal opportunity for athletic trainers.

### Peterson Space Force Base and Schriever SFB Opportunities

Peterson and Schriever Space Force Bases in the Colorado Springs area employ primarily space operations and intelligence personnel. While fitness programs exist, these installations have significantly smaller populations and limited demand for embedded athletic trainers. Occasional positions in base fitness centers or medical clinics may appear, but they are infrequent.

### U.S. Air Force Academy Athletic Training Roles

The U.S. Air Force Academy in Colorado Springs employs athletic trainers to support its intercollegiate athletics programs. These roles more closely resemble traditional collegiate athletic training than tactical human performance, with a focus on NCAA Division I sports coverage. They represent a viable option if your interest leans toward the sports medicine model rather than embedded military care.

### Why Fort Carson Offers the Most AT Positions in Colorado

Fort Carson's combination of troop population size, H2F designation, and diverse unit composition makes it the clear leader for athletic trainer employment among Colorado military installations. No other installation in the state matches its volume of athletic trainer positions or the breadth of clinical experiences available.
