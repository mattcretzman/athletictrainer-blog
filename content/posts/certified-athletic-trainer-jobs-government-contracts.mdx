# Certified Athletic Trainer Jobs: Government Contract Positions You Didn't Know About


When most certified athletic trainers search for jobs, they look at the familiar channels: university postings, hospital systems, high school districts, outpatient clinics. The search terms are predictable — "athletic trainer jobs near me," "sports medicine positions," "AT openings." And the results tend to cycle through the same categories that CAATE programs prepared them for.

What almost never surfaces in that search — and what athletic training programs almost never mention — is an entire sector of employment that has been growing steadily for years: government contract positions.

The Department of Defense employs a growing number of certified athletic trainers through government contractors, primarily to support programs like the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) system and the Marine Corps' Strength and Mobility Injury Prevention (SMIP) program. These are not military enlistment positions. They are not civil service jobs. They are full-time, civilian employment opportunities through private companies that hold contracts with the federal government to deliver healthcare services on military installations.

If you hold a BOC certification and have never considered this path, this guide explains exactly how it works.


## How Government Contract Employment Works

### The Basic Structure

The federal government — specifically the Department of Defense — needs healthcare professionals to support service members. Rather than hiring every provider as a federal employee, the DoD awards contracts to private companies that recruit, employ, and manage the workforce. These companies are called government contractors or defense contractors.

As an athletic trainer working under a government contract, your employment relationship looks like this:

- **Your employer** is the contracting company (not the Army, not the DoD)
- **Your work location** is a military installation
- **Your clinical work** is directed by the military program structure and the physicians overseeing the unit
- **Your paycheck, benefits, and HR support** come from the contracting company

This model is how the majority of athletic trainers in military healthcare are employed today. It is the same structure used for physicians, physical therapists, nurses, and other healthcare professionals working on military installations.

### Why the Government Uses Contractors

The contractor model gives the Department of Defense flexibility. Contracts can be scaled up as programs expand, positions can be filled faster than through the federal hiring process, and the government can bring in specialized clinical expertise — like certified athletic trainers — without creating permanent civil service positions for every role.

For athletic trainers, this structure means opportunity. As the DoD has invested in human performance programs, the number of contract positions has grown. New installations come online, existing programs add staff, and the pipeline of available positions continues to expand.


## Who Holds These Contracts

### Cognito Systems and the PSI Pipeline

Cognito Systems, a joint venture of Resolution Think and QuarterLine, staffed by PSI, is one of the primary contractors delivering athletic training services to the military. Cognito Systems holds contracts to place certified athletic trainers at military installations across the country, primarily within the H2F program.

When you see an athletic trainer position listed for a military installation, there is a strong chance the employer is a government contractor like Cognito Systems — not the military itself.

### How to Identify Government Contract Positions

Government contract athletic trainer jobs appear in many of the same places you would search for any other AT position — Indeed, LinkedIn, NATA career boards — but they are often listed under the contractor's name rather than under "U.S. Army" or "Department of Defense." This is one reason these positions fly under the radar for so many athletic trainers.

Look for these indicators in job postings:

- The employer is a private company, but the work location is a military installation
- The posting references H2F, SMIP, or "tactical human performance"
- Requirements include BOC certification plus a security clearance or background check
- The description mentions supporting service members, brigades, or military units

If you see these markers, you are looking at a government contract position.


## What Government Contract AT Positions Look Like Day to Day

### Clinical Scope

Athletic trainers working under government contracts in military healthcare practice at the full scope of their BOC certification. This includes musculoskeletal evaluation and assessment, manual therapy and therapeutic modalities, injury prevention programming and movement screening, rehabilitation program design and progression, return-to-duty decision-making in coordination with the medical team, and data collection and outcome tracking.

The clinical autonomy is one of the most significant differences between government contract positions and many civilian AT roles. You are not limited to taping ankles or running a modality protocol. You are the primary musculoskeletal provider for your unit, conducting evaluations, making clinical decisions, and managing a caseload of service members from initial complaint through return to full duty.

### Schedule and Work Environment

The standard schedule for most H2F and SMIP contract positions is Monday through Friday, approximately 6:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. — a 40-hour work week. You are home every night. Weekend work is rare and typically limited to periodic field training exercises.

You work in dedicated performance centers and athletic training facilities on military installations, alongside an interdisciplinary team of physical therapists, strength and conditioning coaches, dietitians, and mental performance specialists.

### Team Structure

One of the defining features of military healthcare AT work is the team-based model. You are not working alone in a training room. The H2F system embeds a full performance team within each brigade, and your daily work involves collaboration, case conferences, and shared treatment plans with other healthcare providers.

This structure provides professional development, clinical mentorship, and a referral network that most traditional AT settings cannot match.


## Government Contract vs. Other Employment Models

### Contract Employee vs. GS (Civil Service) Employee

Some athletic trainer positions on military installations are GS (General Schedule) federal employee roles rather than contract positions. The differences are meaningful:

**GS Positions:**
- You are a federal employee
- You receive federal benefits (Thrift Savings Plan, FEHB health insurance, FERS retirement)
- Hiring goes through USAJobs.gov and can take months
- Pay is determined by the GS scale and locality adjustments
- Positions are less common for athletic trainers and tend to be in medical treatment facilities rather than embedded with units

**Contract Positions:**
- You are employed by a private company
- Benefits come from the contractor (health insurance, retirement plans, PTO)
- Hiring can move faster than the federal process
- Compensation is competitive and varies by location
- Most H2F and SMIP athletic trainer positions fall into this category

Neither model is inherently better — they serve different purposes. But if you are searching specifically for positions embedded with military units and focused on human performance, contract positions are where the volume is.

### Contract Employee vs. Traditional Civilian AT

Compared to working at a university, clinic, or hospital, government contract positions differ in several important ways:

**Stability:** Government contracts typically run for multiple years with option periods. While no job is permanent, contract positions in high-demand programs like H2F carry strong continuity because the Department of Defense has committed long-term funding to these programs.

**Benefits:** Contracting companies offer benefits packages that include health insurance, retirement contributions, and paid time off. The specifics vary by employer, but the packages are competitive with hospital and university benefits.

**Relocation:** If you want to move, government contract positions exist at more than 30 military installations. You can transfer without starting over at a new employer — a level of geographic flexibility that most civilian employers cannot offer.

**Mission:** The work connects directly to national defense. Service members depend on athletic trainers to keep them physically ready for their mission. That sense of purpose is a consistent theme among ATs who have transitioned from civilian settings to military healthcare.


## Common Misconceptions About Government Contract AT Jobs

### "I'd have to join the military"

No. Government contract athletic trainers are civilians. You do not enlist, you do not commission, you do not wear a uniform (beyond whatever professional attire the contractor requires). You live off-base or on-base in civilian housing, and you maintain your civilian lifestyle.

### "It's a temporary gig"

Government contracts are structured as multi-year agreements, often with base periods plus option years that extend the contract's life. The H2F program is a permanent fixture of Army force structure, not a pilot program. Positions carry the stability of any full-time employment in a funded, growing program.

### "I need military experience"

Military experience is not a prerequisite. BOC certification is the core requirement, along with any state licensure mandated by the installation's location. Additional credentials like the TSAC-F (Tactical Strength and Conditioning Facilitator) can strengthen an application, but the path is open to athletic trainers from any clinical background.

### "The pay is lower than civilian jobs"

Compensation for government contract positions is competitive and varies by location. When factoring in the 40-hour work week, consistent schedule, benefits, and the absence of on-call or weekend expectations that many civilian positions carry, the total compensation picture is often more favorable than a position with a higher base salary but significantly more hours.

### "I'll lose my certification"

Your BOC certification and state licensure remain active and valid. Military healthcare AT work counts toward your continuing education requirements, and many contractors support professional development by covering conference attendance and certification costs.


## How to Find and Apply for Government Contract AT Positions

### Where to Search

- **[athletictrainerjob.com](https://www.athletictrainerjob.com):** The primary portal for athletic trainer positions through PSI and Cognito Systems
- **LinkedIn and Indeed:** Search for athletic trainer positions and filter by defense contractors or military installation locations
- **NATA Career Center:** Some government contract positions are listed here
- **Company career pages:** Check the careers section of known defense contractors that provide healthcare staffing

### What You Need to Apply

At minimum, government contract AT positions require:

- **BOC certification** in good standing
- **State licensure** for the state where the installation is located (or eligibility to obtain it)
- **Master's degree** from a CAATE-accredited program (standard for all current BOC-certified ATs)
- **Background check clearance** — most positions require at minimum a favorable background investigation
- **CPR/AED certification** current

Preferred qualifications that strengthen an application include TSAC-F certification, experience with tactical or military populations, experience in data collection and outcomes tracking, and familiarity with interdisciplinary healthcare teams.

### The Application Timeline

The hiring process for government contract positions typically moves faster than federal civil service hiring but may still take several weeks to a few months, depending on background check timelines and contract staffing needs. Starting a conversation early — even before you are ready to move — gives you time to understand the positions available and align your timeline.


## Why This Sector Matters for Athletic Training

The growth of government contract positions represents a structural shift in how athletic trainers are employed. The Department of Defense is not experimenting with athletic trainers — it has built them into the permanent architecture of military healthcare. As these programs mature and expand, the number of positions will continue to grow.

For certified athletic trainers who feel constrained by the traditional job market — limited scope, long hours, professional isolation, stagnant advancement — government contract positions offer a fundamentally different model. Full clinical autonomy, team-based practice, predictable schedules, clear career progression, and meaningful work.

The positions exist. The pipeline is open. The question is whether you know to look for them.
