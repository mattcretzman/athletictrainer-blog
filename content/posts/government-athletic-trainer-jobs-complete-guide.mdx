# Government Athletic Trainer Jobs: Federal Contracts, Military Programs & How to Apply

Most athletic trainers think of their career options in familiar categories: high school, college, professional sports, clinic, industrial. What rarely makes the list — and should — is government employment.

The federal government is one of the largest and fastest-growing employers of athletic trainers in the country. Through military readiness programs, VA healthcare systems, and DoD civilian positions, government AT jobs offer stability, competitive compensation, and a professional environment that many traditional settings cannot match.

This guide breaks down the different types of government athletic trainer positions, how each one works, and how to apply.

## Types of Government Athletic Trainer Jobs

Government AT employment falls into three main categories, each with a different employment structure and application process.

### 1. Federal Contract Positions (Military Programs)

This is the largest and fastest-growing category of government AT employment. Athletic trainers work on military installations through government contracts held by private companies. The AT is an employee of the contracting company, not a direct federal employee, but the work takes place on military bases serving active-duty service members.

The two primary programs are:

**Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) — U.S. Army**

The H2F program embeds athletic trainers into Army brigades as part of an interdisciplinary human performance team. ATs work alongside dietitians, strength and conditioning coaches, cognitive performance specialists, and physical therapists across five domains of human performance.

H2F is expanding to every Army brigade, making it the largest single source of military AT positions. Installations span the continental United States, Alaska, Hawaii, and international locations including Japan, Germany, and South Korea.

**Sports Medicine Injury Prevention (SMIP) — U.S. Marine Corps**

The SMIP program places athletic trainers at Marine Corps installations to provide sports medicine and injury prevention services. Key locations include Camp Lejeune (North Carolina), Camp Pendleton (California), Twentynine Palms (California), and Okinawa (Japan).

Both programs are staffed through Cognito Systems, a joint venture of Resolution Think and QuarterLine, staffed by PSI. Athletic trainers apply through Cognito Systems and are employed as full-time civilian employees of the company.

**What makes contract positions different:**

- You are a civilian employee of the contracting company, not a federal employee
- No enlistment, no uniform, no military obligation
- Standard schedule: Monday through Friday daytime hours
- Benefits provided by the contracting company (health insurance, PTO, retirement)
- Positions available at more than 30 locations worldwide
- Your job can transfer with you if you need to relocate

### 2. GS/Civil Service Positions (Direct Federal Employment)

Some athletic trainer positions exist as direct federal employment under the General Schedule (GS) pay system. These roles are found in:

- **Department of Defense (DoD) civilian positions** at military treatment facilities
- **Military service academies** (West Point, Naval Academy, Air Force Academy)
- **Veterans Affairs (VA) medical centers** providing rehabilitation services

GS positions are posted on USAJobs.gov and follow federal hiring procedures, which include detailed application packages, veterans' preference considerations, and often longer hiring timelines than private-sector roles.

Federal civilian AT positions typically fall in the GS-9 to GS-12 range, depending on the role and location. Benefits include the Federal Employees Health Benefits (FEHB) program, the Thrift Savings Plan (TSP) for retirement, and federal leave policies.

### 3. VA Healthcare Athletic Training

The Department of Veterans Affairs employs athletic trainers in some of its medical centers and rehabilitation programs. These positions focus on veteran populations, including post-deployment rehabilitation, chronic pain management, and wellness programming.

VA AT positions are federal civilian roles and are posted on USAJobs.gov. They offer the standard federal benefits package and operate within VA healthcare facilities rather than military installations.

## Why Contract Positions Are the Fastest Path

For athletic trainers looking to enter military healthcare, federal contract positions through programs like H2F and SMIP represent the most accessible and fastest-growing opportunity. Here is why:

**Scale.** The H2F program alone is expanding to cover every Army brigade. The number of AT positions is growing faster than the qualified applicant pool. By comparison, GS and VA positions are limited in number and open infrequently.

**Speed.** The application and hiring process through Cognito Systems moves faster than the federal hiring pipeline. Federal positions on USAJobs can take months from application to start date due to classification requirements, security clearances, and bureaucratic processes.

**Flexibility.** Contract positions offer the ability to choose from more than 30 locations and transfer between them as life circumstances change. Federal positions tie you to a specific facility.

**Team environment.** H2F and SMIP positions are specifically designed around interdisciplinary teams. ATs work alongside other health and performance professionals every day. Many GS positions are standalone roles without this team structure.

**Work-life balance.** The Monday through Friday daytime hours schedule in H2F and SMIP is built into the program structure. Federal civilian and VA positions may not offer the same schedule consistency.

## Qualifications for Government AT Positions

Regardless of the employment pathway, government athletic trainer positions share common qualification requirements:

### Required

- **BOC Certification** — Board of Certification for the Athletic Trainer. This is non-negotiable for all government AT positions.
- **Master's degree** in athletic training or a closely related field (exercise science, kinesiology, sports medicine).
- **State licensure** — or eligibility for licensure in the state where the installation or facility is located. Cognito Systems assists with licensure for positions in new states.
- **Clinical experience** — most positions require at least one to two years of clinical AT experience.

### Helpful but Not Required

- Experience working with military or tactical populations
- Familiarity with performance optimization and human performance programming
- Experience with data collection, outcomes tracking, and evidence-based practice
- CPR/AED certification (often required but easily obtained)

### Security

Most military installation positions require the ability to pass a background check. This is a standard process and does not require prior military service or security clearance.

## How to Apply for Each Type

### Contract Positions (H2F/SMIP through Cognito Systems)

1. Visit [athletictrainerjob.com](https://athletictrainerjob.com) to view open positions
2. Select positions by location, program, or role type
3. Submit your application through the Cognito Systems portal
4. The interview process includes both evaluation and education — the recruiting team wants to make sure candidates fully understand the role before accepting
5. Onboarding includes base orientation, team integration, and program-specific training

### GS/Civil Service Positions

1. Create a profile on [USAJobs.gov](https://usajobs.gov)
2. Search for "athletic trainer" or "exercise physiologist" (some AT roles are classified under different position series)
3. Prepare a federal resume (longer and more detailed than a private-sector resume)
4. Apply to specific positions and complete any required questionnaires
5. Be prepared for a longer timeline — weeks to months for the hiring process

### VA Positions

1. Follow the same USAJobs.gov process as GS positions
2. Search specifically within the Department of Veterans Affairs
3. VA positions may also be posted on the VA careers website

## The Bigger Picture

Government employment of athletic trainers is not a niche opportunity — it is becoming a major segment of the profession. The expansion of H2F across the Army, the growth of SMIP in the Marine Corps, and emerging pilot programs in the Air Force and Navy all point to sustained, long-term demand for qualified ATs in military healthcare.

For athletic trainers working in traditional settings — facing long hours, solo coverage, and limited advancement — government positions offer an alternative that respects the profession and the professionals in it.

The positions are real, the demand is growing, and the application process is more straightforward than most ATs expect.
