# Fort Cavazos Civilian Jobs: Athletic Trainer Positions and How to Apply


Fort Cavazos is the largest active-duty armored military installation in the free world. Located in Central Texas between Killeen and Copperas Cove, the installation spans approximately 214,000 acres and supports more than 45,000 active-duty soldiers, making it one of the most significant military installations in the United States — and one of the largest civilian employers in the region.

If you are searching for "Fort Cavazos jobs" or "civilian jobs at Fort Cavazos," you are looking at a community where the military and civilian workforce are deeply interconnected. Thousands of civilian employees work on the installation in healthcare, administration, logistics, education, maintenance, and technical services. Among the growing categories of civilian employment at Fort Cavazos: healthcare professionals supporting the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program, including certified athletic trainers.

This guide covers what you need to know about civilian employment at Fort Cavazos — with a specific focus on athletic trainer positions and how to pursue them.


## Understanding Civilian Employment at Military Installations

### How Civilians Work on Military Bases

Military installations are not staffed exclusively by uniformed service members. A substantial portion of the workforce is civilian, and those civilian positions fall into two primary categories:

**Government civilian employees (GS/GG):** These are federal employees hired through the Office of Personnel Management. They are paid on the General Schedule (GS) pay scale, receive federal benefits, and are hired through USAJobs.gov. Positions range from administrative support to specialized healthcare roles.

**Government contractor employees:** These are employees of private companies that hold contracts with the Department of Defense to provide specific services. The employee works on the installation and supports the military mission, but their employer is the contracting company — not the federal government. Benefits, pay, and HR processes come through the contractor.

Both categories represent stable, professional employment. The distinction matters primarily for how you apply, who manages your employment, and the structure of your benefits.

### Why Fort Cavazos Has So Many Civilian Jobs

Fort Cavazos is home to III Corps headquarters, the 1st Cavalry Division, elements of the 1st Armored Division, and numerous supporting units. The scale of the installation — tens of thousands of soldiers conducting armored and combined-arms training operations — requires an equally large support infrastructure.

Healthcare alone drives significant civilian employment. Darnall Army Medical Center, located on the installation, is a full-service hospital serving the military community. Beyond the hospital, the H2F program has embedded performance teams — including athletic trainers, physical therapists, strength coaches, and dietitians — within each brigade on the installation.

Add in education (Department of Defense schools, the on-post university center), logistics and supply chain management, information technology, facilities maintenance, child development centers, and morale and recreation programs, and you have a civilian workforce that numbers in the thousands.


## Athletic Trainer Positions at Fort Cavazos

### The H2F Program at Fort Cavazos

Fort Cavazos is one of the Army's largest H2F sites. With multiple Brigade Combat Teams on the installation, the demand for certified athletic trainers is substantial. Each brigade's H2F performance team includes athletic trainers who work directly with service members on injury prevention, musculoskeletal evaluation, treatment, and rehabilitation.

Athletic trainer positions at Fort Cavazos are primarily staffed through government contractors. Cognito Systems, a joint venture of Resolution Think and QuarterLine, staffed by PSI, is one of the contractors placing athletic trainers into these roles.

### What the Role Looks Like

As an athletic trainer at Fort Cavazos, your work centers on the physical readiness of the soldiers in your assigned unit. A typical day runs from approximately daytime hours, Monday through Friday — a 40-hour work week.

Daily responsibilities include:

- **Morning movement preparation:** Conducting warm-up sessions, movement screens, and injury prevention programming before physical training
- **Walk-in evaluations:** Assessing musculoskeletal complaints from soldiers who report to the performance center with acute or chronic issues
- **Treatment and rehabilitation:** Providing manual therapy, therapeutic exercise, modalities, and progressive rehabilitation plans
- **Interdisciplinary coordination:** Collaborating with physical therapists, strength coaches, dietitians, and unit medical officers on treatment plans and return-to-duty decisions
- **Documentation and data collection:** Recording clinical encounters, tracking outcomes, and contributing to unit readiness metrics

The clinical scope is broad. Athletic trainers in H2F practice at the full extent of their BOC certification, with autonomy in clinical decision-making that many civilian settings do not afford.

### Requirements for Athletic Trainer Positions

To be eligible for athletic trainer positions at Fort Cavazos through a government contractor:

- **BOC certification** in good standing
- **Texas state licensure** through the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR), or eligibility to obtain it
- **Master's degree** from a CAATE-accredited program
- **Background check clearance** — a favorable background investigation is standard for installation access
- **CPR/AED certification** current

Preferred qualifications include TSAC-F certification, experience with tactical or military populations, and familiarity with electronic health records and outcome tracking.

Military experience is not required. Athletic trainers from sports medicine clinics, universities, hospitals, and industrial settings have all successfully transitioned into H2F positions.

### How to Apply

Athletic trainer positions at Fort Cavazos through Cognito Systems are posted on [athletictrainerjob.com](https://www.athletictrainerjob.com). You can also find listings on LinkedIn, Indeed, and NATA career boards — look for the contractor name and the Fort Cavazos location.

The application process typically involves submitting your resume and credentials, interviewing with the hiring team, completing a background investigation, and obtaining Texas licensure if you do not already hold it.

The timeline from application to start date varies, but the process generally moves faster than federal GS hiring.


## Living in Central Texas: The Fort Cavazos Area

### The Killeen-Temple-Fort Cavazos Metro

The Killeen-Temple-Fort Cavazos Metropolitan Statistical Area has a population of approximately 475,000. Killeen is the city immediately adjacent to the installation's main gates. Temple, about 30 miles to the east, offers additional amenities and a slightly different community feel. Copperas Cove and Harker Heights round out the primary residential areas.

### Cost of Living

Central Texas offers a cost of living that runs 15-20 percent below the national average. Housing is particularly affordable relative to major metro areas. Whether you are renting or buying, your housing dollar stretches further in the Killeen-Temple area than in most regions where military installations are located.

Texas does not levy a state income tax, which provides additional take-home pay compared to states with income tax obligations. For athletic trainers comparing compensation across positions in different states, this factor is significant.

### Housing Options

**Killeen:** The closest city to the main gates. Killeen offers the most direct commute and a wide range of housing from apartments to single-family homes. Neighborhoods vary, so researching specific areas is recommended.

**Harker Heights:** Adjacent to Killeen, Harker Heights offers newer residential development, strong schools, and a slightly suburban feel while remaining close to the installation.

**Copperas Cove:** West of the installation, Copperas Cove provides a smaller-town atmosphere with competitive home prices and a community closely tied to the military.

**Temple:** About 30 minutes east, Temple offers more dining and retail variety, a growing healthcare sector (Baylor Scott & White's major campus is here), and a feel that is somewhat less military-centric. Some professionals prefer the slight separation while still maintaining a manageable commute.

**Belton:** A smaller community near Temple, Belton offers a historic downtown, proximity to Belton Lake, and the campus of the University of Mary Hardin-Baylor.

### Recreation and Quality of Life

Central Texas is not a major metro area, but it offers meaningful quality-of-life advantages for outdoor-oriented professionals:

- **Belton Lake and Stillhouse Hollow Lake** are both within 20-30 minutes and offer fishing, boating, swimming, and lakeside parks
- **Dana Peak Park** and **Stillhouse Hollow recreation areas** provide hiking and mountain biking trails
- **Austin** is approximately 60 miles south — accessible for weekend outings, live music, dining, and cultural events
- **Waco** is about 50 miles north, home to Magnolia Market and a growing downtown scene
- The area receives more than 230 days of sunshine per year, with mild winters that allow year-round outdoor activity

For athletic trainers who value an affordable cost of living, access to outdoor recreation, and proximity to a major city without the cost and congestion of living in one, Central Texas delivers.

### The Military Community

Living near a large military installation means being part of a community where military families and civilian employees share space. The culture is welcoming, the pace is manageable, and the services available on-post — commissary, exchange, recreation facilities — are accessible to employees with installation access.

For newcomers, the military community connection can ease the transition. Many of your colleagues and neighbors will be in similar situations — professionals who relocated for a meaningful career opportunity and found a community that supports the lifestyle they wanted.

## GS Positions vs. Contractor Positions at Fort Cavazos

If you are exploring civilian employment at Fort Cavazos, you will encounter both GS and contractor positions. Here is a quick comparison:

**GS (Government Civilian) Positions:**
- Applied for through USAJobs.gov
- Federal employee status with federal benefits
- Pay determined by GS scale plus locality adjustment
- Longer hiring timeline (often several months)
- Subject to government shutdown furloughs

**Contractor Positions:**
- Applied for through the contracting company (e.g., Cognito Systems via athletictrainerjob.com)
- Employee of the contractor with contractor-provided benefits
- Competitive compensation that varies by role and location
- Generally faster hiring process
- Not subject to government shutdown furloughs (though contract funding can be affected by budget disruptions)

For athletic trainer positions specifically, the majority of H2F roles at Fort Cavazos are contractor positions. GS athletic trainer positions do exist within Darnall Army Medical Center, but they are fewer in number and less frequently posted.
