## Fort Bliss: A Unique Setting for Athletic Trainer Careers

Fort Bliss, stretching across more than 1.1 million acres of desert terrain in west Texas and southern New Mexico, ranks as one of the largest military installations in the United States by total area. Located adjacent to El Paso, Texas, the installation serves as the home of the 1st Armored Division and a growing number of units that depend on the Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program to keep service members performing at their best.

For athletic trainers looking to build a career in military healthcare, Fort Bliss offers a combination of clinical challenge, professional stability, and a lifestyle that surprises many first-time visitors. The desert Southwest is not what most people picture when they think of Army installations, and that distinction is precisely what makes Fort Bliss worth a closer look.

Cognito Systems, a joint venture of Resolution Think and QuarterLine, staffed by PSI, manages athletic trainer positions at Fort Bliss as part of the H2F program. These positions place certified athletic trainers directly within operational units, working as integrated members of interdisciplinary performance teams.

## The H2F Program at Fort Bliss

### How H2F Functions Within the 1st Armored Division

The 1st Armored Division, known as "Old Ironsides," has a storied history and a demanding present-day mission. Service members assigned to Fort Bliss train in an environment that tests endurance, heat tolerance, and physical resilience in ways that differ markedly from installations in temperate climates. The H2F program at Fort Bliss accounts for these environmental factors, and athletic trainers play a central role in helping soldiers perform safely and effectively under desert conditions.

H2F performance teams at Fort Bliss are embedded at the brigade level, meaning athletic trainers work closely with a defined population of service members over time. This continuity of care allows practitioners to build relationships, track progress, and develop individualized prevention and rehabilitation strategies that would be impossible in a rotating clinic model.

### The Interdisciplinary Team Structure

Each H2F performance team includes athletic trainers, strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists. This team-based approach ensures that no single provider is expected to address every aspect of a soldier's health and performance. Athletic trainers focus on their core competencies — musculoskeletal assessment, injury prevention, therapeutic intervention, and return-to-duty protocols — while collaborating daily with colleagues who bring complementary expertise.

The team structure also means built-in professional support. Athletic trainers at Fort Bliss work alongside peers and allied health professionals, sharing clinical observations, discussing complex cases, and developing coordinated care plans. This collaborative environment fosters professional growth in ways that isolated practice settings simply cannot match.

## What a Typical Day Looks Like

### Schedule and Work-Life Balance

Athletic trainer positions at Fort Bliss follow a consistent Monday through Friday schedule, typically from daytime hours The 40-hour workweek is a defining feature of H2F positions and one that distinguishes military healthcare roles from the demanding schedules common in collegiate and professional sports.

There are no evening games, no weekend travel, and no seasonal spikes that push practitioners into 60- or 70-hour weeks. Athletic trainers at Fort Bliss go home every night. They have their evenings free. Their weekends belong to them. For practitioners who have spent years working split shifts and holiday schedules, this consistency represents a meaningful change in quality of life.

### Morning Through Afternoon

The day typically begins with the unit's physical training period. Athletic trainers observe movement quality, provide sideline coverage for higher-risk training activities, and conduct informal screenings. The morning PT window is one of the most valuable touchpoints in the H2F model because it places athletic trainers in direct contact with the full unit population, not just those who self-report injuries.

Following PT, the schedule shifts to individual assessments and treatments. Service members with musculoskeletal complaints, those recovering from injuries, or those on physical profiles receive hands-on evaluation and care. Treatment sessions may include manual therapy, therapeutic exercise, functional movement retraining, taping, or referral coordination with physical therapists and medical providers.

The afternoon often includes documentation, team coordination meetings, and program development. Athletic trainers contribute to unit-level injury surveillance, identify trends, and design targeted prevention initiatives. At Fort Bliss, heat-related injury prevention and load carriage optimization are frequent areas of focus given the installation's environment and training tempo.

## Environmental Considerations and Clinical Challenges

### Working in the Desert

Fort Bliss sits in the Chihuahuan Desert, and the climate shapes both the training environment and the clinical challenges that athletic trainers encounter. Summer temperatures regularly exceed 100 degrees Fahrenheit, and the dry air, high altitude (approximately 3,700 feet), and intense sun exposure create conditions that demand careful attention to hydration, heat acclimatization, and exertional heat illness prevention.

Athletic trainers at Fort Bliss develop specialized knowledge in environmental physiology that serves them throughout their careers. Understanding wet bulb globe temperature readings, work-rest cycles, and the physiological adaptations required for sustained performance in extreme heat is not just academic at Fort Bliss — it is part of daily practice.

### Altitude and Terrain

The installation's elevation and vast desert terrain also influence musculoskeletal injury patterns. Service members conducting field exercises over uneven, rocky ground at altitude experience different stressors than those training on flat, temperate terrain. Athletic trainers at Fort Bliss become adept at recognizing and addressing the specific lower extremity injuries, load carriage issues, and overuse conditions that arise in this environment.

## Qualifications for Athletic Trainer Positions at Fort Bliss

### Education and Certification

Candidates must hold at minimum a bachelor's degree from an accredited athletic training program, with a master's degree preferred. Current Board of Certification (BOC) certification is required, along with eligibility for state licensure in Texas. Athletic trainers must maintain CPR and AED certification throughout their employment.

### Experience and Skills

Experience in musculoskeletal assessment, rehabilitation, and injury prevention with active populations is essential. Familiarity with evidence-based therapeutic techniques and functional movement assessment is expected. While prior military or government healthcare experience is a plus, it is not a prerequisite — Cognito Systems supports athletic trainers transitioning from a variety of professional backgrounds.

### Security and Background Requirements

All positions require the ability to obtain and maintain the appropriate level of security clearance. A valid driver's license and successful completion of a background investigation are standard requirements for employment at Fort Bliss.

## Living in El Paso, Texas

### A City That Defies Expectations

El Paso consistently ranks among the safest large cities in the United States, and its cost of living falls well below the national average. For athletic trainers relocating from coastal cities or major metropolitan areas, the financial impact is immediately noticeable — housing, groceries, and daily expenses are significantly more affordable.

The city is home to roughly 700,000 residents and carries a rich cross-cultural identity shaped by its position on the U.S.-Mexico border. El Paso's food scene reflects this heritage, with restaurants ranging from legendary Tex-Mex and authentic Mexican cuisine to a growing number of craft breweries and contemporary dining options.

### Outdoor Recreation

With more than 300 days of sunshine per year, El Paso is built for outdoor living. Franklin Mountains State Park, the largest urban park in the contiguous United States, sits within the city limits and offers hiking, mountain biking, and rock climbing. The Hueco Tanks State Historic Site, located about 30 miles east, is internationally renowned for bouldering.

The Rio Grande runs through the region, and the surrounding desert landscape offers opportunities for trail running, road cycling, and exploration that feel a world apart from typical urban recreation. For athletic trainers who value an active lifestyle outside of work, El Paso delivers consistently.

### Proximity and Travel

El Paso sits at the intersection of Texas, New Mexico, and Mexico. Las Cruces, New Mexico, is approximately 45 minutes north and offers additional dining, shopping, and cultural venues. The ski resorts of Ruidoso and Cloudcroft in the Sacramento Mountains are roughly three hours northeast, providing a mountain escape during winter months.

El Paso International Airport connects the city to major hubs across the country, making travel straightforward despite the city's western location. Ciudad Juarez, Mexico, is immediately across the border, and cross-border visits for dining and cultural experiences are a routine part of life for many El Paso residents.

### Community and Family Life

El Paso's strong military community, anchored by Fort Bliss, creates a welcoming environment for newcomers. Neighborhoods on the east and northeast sides of the city are particularly popular with installation-affiliated families, offering newer construction, good schools, and easy commutes to the post.

The city's pace of life is notably relaxed compared to larger Texas metros. The combination of affordable housing, safe neighborhoods, abundant sunshine, and a tight-knit community creates conditions that many athletic trainers and their families find genuinely appealing once they experience it firsthand.
