## Fort Drum and the 10th Mountain Division: A Career Unlike Any Other

Fort Drum sits in the North Country of New York State, near the city of Watertown and within reach of the Adirondack Mountains, Lake Ontario, and the Thousand Islands region. It serves as home to the 10th Mountain Division (Light Infantry), one of the most frequently deployed divisions in the United States Army. For athletic trainers seeking a career that combines clinical purpose with a setting that feels genuinely different from the typical sports medicine path, Fort Drum delivers on both counts.

The installation's mission, its climate, and its location create a professional environment that challenges and rewards athletic trainers in ways that few other duty stations can replicate. Working with mountain infantry soldiers in a region known for harsh winters and rugged terrain means that the clinical demands are real, the work matters, and the experience gained here carries significant professional weight.

Cognito Systems, a joint venture of Resolution Think and QuarterLine, staffed by PSI, manages athletic trainer positions at Fort Drum through the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program. These positions embed certified athletic trainers directly into the operational units of the 10th Mountain Division, providing daily access to the soldiers who depend on their expertise.

## The H2F Program at Fort Drum

### Supporting the 10th Mountain Division

The 10th Mountain Division specializes in operations across difficult terrain and in austere environments. Service members assigned here train for mountain warfare, cold weather operations, and rapid deployment scenarios that demand exceptional physical fitness and resilience. The H2F program at Fort Drum is designed to meet these specific demands, embedding interdisciplinary performance teams at the brigade level to support soldier readiness year-round.

Athletic trainers within these teams serve as the primary point of contact for musculoskeletal health. They assess injuries, design rehabilitation protocols, implement prevention programs, and advise unit leadership on training modifications that reduce injury risk without compromising readiness. At Fort Drum, where the physical demands on service members are consistently high, the athletic trainer's role carries particular weight.

### What Makes the 10th Mountain Division Different

Working with light infantry soldiers presents clinical challenges that differ from those encountered at mechanized or armored installations. The 10th Mountain Division's soldiers carry heavy loads over long distances on foot, often across uneven and challenging terrain. They conduct training in snow, ice, and sub-zero temperatures. They execute rapid movements that stress the musculoskeletal system in patterns specific to dismounted operations.

Athletic trainers at Fort Drum develop expertise in load carriage optimization, cold weather injury prevention, lower extremity overuse conditions, and the unique demands of rucking over varied terrain. This specialized knowledge base is built through daily practice, not classroom theory, and it distinguishes Fort Drum athletic trainers within the broader profession.

### The Team Model

Each H2F performance team at Fort Drum includes athletic trainers working alongside strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists. The team-based approach ensures comprehensive coverage of the performance spectrum, and it provides athletic trainers with a collaborative support structure that enhances both patient outcomes and professional satisfaction.

Athletic trainers are not expected to be everything to every soldier. The team model allows each practitioner to focus on their domain of expertise while coordinating seamlessly with colleagues who address complementary aspects of soldier health. Daily team meetings, shared documentation systems, and co-located workspaces make this collaboration natural rather than forced.

## Daily Life as an Athletic Trainer at Fort Drum

### The Schedule

Athletic trainer positions at Fort Drum follow a Monday through Friday schedule, typically running from daytime hours The 40-hour workweek provides a level of consistency and predictability that is rare in the athletic training profession. There are no weekend games, no late-night travel, and no seasonal schedule swings. Athletic trainers go home every night with their evenings and weekends intact.

For practitioners transitioning from collegiate or professional sports settings where 50- to 70-hour weeks were the norm, this schedule represents a fundamental shift in work-life balance. The early start means early finishes, leaving afternoons free for personal pursuits, family time, or enjoying the considerable outdoor recreation that the North Country offers.

### A Day With the 10th Mountain Division

Mornings begin with the unit's physical training session. Athletic trainers are present on the training grounds, observing movement quality, providing immediate assessment for acute complaints, and building the kind of trust that only comes from being consistently present when soldiers are working hardest. This visibility during PT is one of the H2F model's greatest strengths — it removes the barrier between provider and patient and creates a proactive care dynamic.

After the PT window, athletic trainers transition to scheduled treatment sessions. Service members recovering from injuries, those on profiles, or those flagged during movement screenings receive individualized care. Treatments span the full scope of athletic training practice: manual therapy, therapeutic exercise, neuromuscular re-education, taping and bracing, and functional progression protocols.

The latter portion of the day typically involves documentation, coordination with the broader H2F team, and programmatic work. Athletic trainers at Fort Drum often develop injury prevention initiatives specific to the unit's training cycle, addressing known risk factors associated with upcoming field exercises, seasonal conditions, or historical injury patterns within the brigade.

### Cold Weather and Seasonal Considerations

Fort Drum receives significant snowfall — often exceeding 100 inches annually — and winter temperatures regularly drop well below zero. For athletic trainers, this means that cold weather injury prevention is not an occasional topic but a persistent clinical reality. Understanding cold weather injuries, appropriate layering and equipment, warm-up protocols in frigid conditions, and the physiological effects of sustained cold exposure becomes second nature for practitioners stationed here.

The seasonal variation also introduces clinical diversity. Summer training brings heat-related considerations, fall rucking season increases lower extremity loads, and winter operations demand attention to frostbite risk, reduced mobility from heavy clothing, and the biomechanical changes that snow and ice impose on movement patterns. Athletic trainers at Fort Drum develop a year-round clinical perspective that accounts for environmental factors in ways that temperate-climate positions never require.

## Qualifications for H2F Athletic Trainer Positions

### Education and Certification

Applicants must hold a bachelor's degree from an accredited athletic training program at minimum, with a master's degree preferred. Current certification from the Board of Certification (BOC) is required, as is eligibility for licensure as an athletic trainer in New York State. CPR and AED certification must be current and maintained throughout employment.

### Professional Experience

Demonstrated experience in musculoskeletal assessment, injury rehabilitation, and prevention programming with active populations is expected. Athletic trainers with backgrounds in high-demand settings — whether collegiate athletics, sports medicine clinics, or occupational health — tend to transition well into the H2F environment. Prior military healthcare experience is valued but not required, and Cognito Systems supports practitioners coming from a range of professional backgrounds.

### Security and Administrative Requirements

Candidates must be able to obtain and maintain the appropriate security clearance for work on a military installation. A valid driver's license and successful background check are standard prerequisites.

## Living in the North Country

### Watertown and the Surrounding Region

Watertown, the closest city to Fort Drum, has a population of roughly 25,000 and serves as the commercial hub of Jefferson County. The city offers the essential amenities — grocery stores, restaurants, healthcare facilities, and retail — while maintaining the character of a small northern New York community. Housing costs in the Watertown area are notably affordable, with both rental and purchase prices well below state and national averages.

For athletic trainers accustomed to the high housing costs of metropolitan areas or college towns, the North Country's affordability is a significant practical benefit. The savings on cost of living, combined with competitive compensation from Cognito Systems, create a financial picture that compares favorably to many positions in higher-cost regions.

### Outdoor Recreation That Rivals Anywhere in the Northeast

The North Country's location is, by any objective measure, one of its greatest assets for people who value outdoor activity. The Adirondack Park, the largest publicly protected area in the contiguous United States at over six million acres, begins roughly 30 minutes east of Fort Drum. It offers world-class hiking, kayaking, canoeing, fishing, backcountry camping, and skiing.

Lake Ontario, one of the Great Lakes, sits to the west and provides opportunities for fishing — including renowned smallmouth bass and salmon fisheries — boating, and waterfront recreation. The Thousand Islands region, where the St. Lawrence River meets Lake Ontario, is one of the most scenic areas in the northeastern United States and sits within an easy drive of Fort Drum.

Winter transforms the North Country into a destination for cross-country skiing, snowshoeing, ice fishing, and snowmobiling. Tug Hill Plateau, located between Fort Drum and the Adirondacks, receives some of the heaviest snowfall east of the Rockies and is a magnet for winter recreation enthusiasts.

### Acknowledging the Location Honestly

Fort Drum is not next to a major city. The nearest large metropolitan area, Syracuse, is approximately 70 miles to the south. For practitioners who require easy access to big-city nightlife, professional sports, or large-scale cultural institutions, this distance is worth considering carefully.

However, many athletic trainers who arrive at Fort Drum with reservations about the location find that the North Country wins them over. The tight-knit community, the extraordinary natural surroundings, the low cost of living, and the absence of traffic and congestion create a quality of life that is genuinely difficult to replicate elsewhere. The area attracts people who value authenticity, outdoor adventure, and a pace of life that allows room to breathe.

For those willing to embrace it, Fort Drum's location is not a limitation — it is part of what makes the experience distinctive.

### Community and Family Considerations

The Fort Drum military community is deeply integrated into the North Country's fabric. Local businesses, schools, and organizations are accustomed to welcoming newcomers and supporting families affiliated with the installation. School districts in the area, including Watertown, Carthage, and Indian River, serve military-connected students regularly and understand the rhythms of military life.

The close-knit nature of the community means that professional and social networks develop quickly. Athletic trainers new to the area often find that the combination of the H2F team environment and the welcoming local community accelerates the adjustment period considerably.

## Career Development and Professional Value

A tour at Fort Drum builds clinical credentials that stand out. Working with one of the Army's most active divisions, in an environment that demands year-round environmental awareness and specialized injury prevention knowledge, produces athletic trainers with a depth of experience that employers across the profession recognize and value.

Cognito Systems offers competitive compensation and benefits for athletic trainer positions at Fort Drum. The organization supports continuing education and professional development, and the H2F program's emphasis on evidence-based practice ensures that athletic trainers stay current with best practices in their field.

The stability of the position — 40-hour weeks, predictable schedules, team-based practice, meaningful work — provides a foundation that allows athletic trainers to invest in their careers without sacrificing their personal lives. That balance is increasingly rare in the profession, and it is one of the H2F program's strongest recruitment advantages.
