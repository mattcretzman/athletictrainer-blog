Certification and credentialing requirements for athletic trainers vary by employer, state, and setting. For athletic trainers exploring military healthcare careers — particularly positions within the Army's H2F program or the Marine Corps SMIP program — the credentialing requirements are specific, non-negotiable, and in some cases differ from what traditional employers require.

This guide covers exactly what certifications and credentials military athletic trainer programs require, how state licensure works when you are practicing on a federal installation, what continuing education obligations look like, and how the credentialing process compares to traditional AT employment.

## The Foundation: BOC Certification

The Board of Certification (BOC) credential is the baseline requirement for every athletic trainer position in military healthcare. There are no exceptions to this.

### What BOC Certification Means

The BOC certification (ATC credential) verifies that an athletic trainer has completed an accredited professional education program and passed the BOC certification examination. It is the nationally recognized standard for entry into the athletic training profession and the credential that military healthcare contracts reference as a mandatory requirement.

### Maintaining BOC Certification

BOC certification is not a one-time achievement. To maintain the ATC credential, athletic trainers must complete continuing education requirements within each reporting period. The current BOC requirement is 50 continuing education units (CEUs) every two-year reporting period, with specific category requirements that include:

- **Evidence-Based Practice (EBP)** — A minimum number of CEUs must fall in this category
- **BOC-approved programs** — CEUs must come from approved providers
- **Professional development** — Activities that expand clinical competence and professional knowledge

Athletic trainers in military healthcare positions are expected to maintain active, unrestricted BOC certification throughout their employment. Allowing certification to lapse is a disqualifying event — it is not something that can be remedied retroactively while remaining in the position.

### How Military Programs Verify BOC Status

Government contracts specify that contractor personnel must maintain required certifications as a condition of employment. Cognito Systems, a joint venture of Resolution Think and QuarterLine, staffed by PSI, verifies BOC certification status during the hiring process and monitors it throughout employment. Athletic trainers are responsible for ensuring their certification remains current and for providing documentation of renewal to their employer.

## State Licensure Requirements

State licensure is the second critical credentialing component for military healthcare athletic trainers. While BOC certification is a national credential, state licensure governs the legal authority to practice as an athletic trainer in a specific state.

### How State Licensure Works on Military Installations

Military installations are federal property, but the state licensure requirement still applies for contractor employees. Athletic trainers employed by government contractors like Cognito Systems must hold a current, unrestricted AT license in the state where their assigned installation is located.

This means:

- An athletic trainer assigned to Fort Liberty, North Carolina, needs a North Carolina AT license
- An athletic trainer assigned to Fort Cavazos, Texas, needs a Texas AT license
- An athletic trainer assigned to Joint Base Lewis-McChord, Washington, needs a Washington AT license

### Licensure for OCONUS Positions

For positions outside the continental United States — such as Germany, Japan, South Korea, or Hawaii — the licensure situation may differ. OCONUS positions may require licensure in the state where the contractor's corporate office is located or may follow different credentialing pathways. Specific requirements are outlined in individual position descriptions and clarified during the hiring process.

### Interstate Licensure Compact

The Athletic Trainer Licensure Compact has been a significant development for the profession. States that have joined the compact allow athletic trainers licensed in one compact member state to practice in other member states without obtaining an additional license. For athletic trainers considering military healthcare positions that may involve relocating to a different state, the compact can simplify the licensure process.

However, not all states have joined the compact as of 2026. Athletic trainers should verify whether their current state of licensure and their target installation's state are both compact members. If not, a separate state license application will be required.

### Licensure Application Timelines

State AT licensure applications can take anywhere from two weeks to several months, depending on the state. Athletic trainers who receive a position offer should begin the licensure process immediately. PSI's onboarding team assists candidates with understanding state-specific requirements and timelines, but the responsibility for completing the application rests with the athletic trainer.

## BLS and Emergency Certifications

Beyond the BOC credential and state licensure, military healthcare athletic trainer positions require specific emergency certifications.

### Basic Life Support (BLS)

Current BLS certification from the American Heart Association (AHA) or an equivalent provider is required for all military AT positions. BLS certification must be maintained throughout employment, with recertification completed before expiration.

### AED Certification

Automated External Defibrillator (AED) certification is typically required alongside BLS. Most BLS courses include AED training as a standard component.

### First Aid

Current first aid certification may be required depending on the specific contract and position. Like BLS, this is a straightforward certification that athletic trainers should keep current regardless of their employment setting.

## Educational Requirements

Military healthcare AT positions have specific educational requirements that reflect the profession's evolving standards.

### Master's Degree Requirement

The standard educational requirement for military athletic trainer positions is a master's degree in athletic training or a closely related field from a CAATE-accredited program. This aligns with the profession's transition to the professional master's degree as the entry-level credential.

Athletic trainers who hold a bachelor's degree in athletic training and earned their BOC certification under previous educational standards may still qualify for some positions, depending on the specific contract requirements and their professional experience. However, the master's degree has become the prevailing standard for new hires.

### CAATE Accreditation

The Commission on Accreditation of Athletic Training Education (CAATE) accredits athletic training programs. Graduation from a CAATE-accredited program is a requirement for BOC certification eligibility and, by extension, for military healthcare positions. Athletic trainers should ensure they can document their graduation from an accredited program if asked during the credentialing process.

## What H2F Specifically Requires

The Army's H2F program has credential requirements defined in the contract's Performance Work Statement (PWS). While specific language may vary between contract iterations, the standard H2F athletic trainer requirements include:

- **BOC certification (ATC)** — Active and unrestricted
- **State licensure** — In the state where the installation is located
- **Master's degree** — In athletic training or a related field
- **BLS/AED certification** — Current and maintained
- **Minimum experience** — Typically one to two years of clinical experience post-certification
- **Background investigation** — Favorable NACI or equivalent security clearance

### Additional H2F Preferences

While not always mandatory, the following credentials and qualifications strengthen an H2F application:

- **CSCS (Certified Strength and Conditioning Specialist)** — Valued given the performance focus of H2F
- **Manual therapy certifications** — Instrument-assisted soft tissue mobilization, dry needling (where state practice acts allow), and other manual techniques
- **Tactical athlete experience** — Prior work with military, law enforcement, or fire/rescue populations
- **Experience with electronic medical records** — Familiarity with military health systems like AHLTA or MHS Genesis

## What SMIP Specifically Requires

The Marine Corps SMIP program has similar baseline requirements but may include variations:

- **BOC certification (ATC)** — Active and unrestricted
- **State licensure** — Applicable to the installation's state
- **Master's degree** — In athletic training or related field
- **BLS/AED certification** — Current
- **Clinical experience** — Minimum experience requirements as specified in the contract

SMIP positions may place additional emphasis on injury prevention programming experience and population-level health screening competencies, reflecting the program's focus on reducing musculoskeletal injury rates across Marine units.

## How Military Credentialing Compares to Traditional Employers

Athletic trainers coming from traditional settings will find that military healthcare credentialing requirements are more structured and more strictly enforced than what many civilian employers require.

### More Structured Than Most Civilian Employers

In many traditional settings — particularly high schools and small colleges — the credentialing process can be informal. An athletic trainer presents their ATC card and state license during onboarding, and that may be the last time anyone checks. Military healthcare contracts include specific credentialing requirements that are monitored and verified on an ongoing basis.

### Background Investigation

The federal background investigation is the most significant additional requirement that military healthcare positions carry compared to traditional AT roles. This process verifies criminal history, employment history, financial responsibility, and other factors relevant to working on a military installation. The investigation takes time — weeks to months — and is a non-negotiable step in the hiring process.

### Credentialing Timelines

The combination of BOC verification, state licensure, BLS certification, educational verification, and background investigation means that the credentialing timeline for military healthcare positions is typically longer than for traditional AT roles. Athletic trainers should expect the process to take several weeks to a few months from offer to start date.

### The Benefit of Structure

While the credentialing requirements are more demanding, they serve a purpose beyond compliance. The structured credentialing process ensures that every athletic trainer practicing in military healthcare meets a consistent standard of qualification. This protects both the service members receiving care and the athletic trainers providing it.

## Continuing Education in Military Healthcare

Maintaining credentials is an ongoing obligation, and military healthcare positions support that obligation in meaningful ways.

### Employer Support for CEUs

Cognito Systems provides continuing education support for athletic trainers. This may include funding for CEU courses, conference attendance, and professional development activities. The specific level of support varies by position and contract, but the commitment to professional development is a consistent feature of military healthcare employment.

### Military-Relevant CE Opportunities

Athletic trainers in military healthcare have access to continuing education opportunities that are unique to their setting:

- **Tactical strength and conditioning** programs and certifications
- **Military-specific injury prevention** research and training
- **Interdisciplinary team collaboration** training
- **Leadership development** programs within the contractor organization
- **Department of Defense health system** training

These opportunities expand an AT's professional scope in ways that are difficult to access in traditional settings.

### Professional Growth Within the Role

The military healthcare environment itself serves as a continuing education experience. Working alongside physical therapists, occupational therapists, strength and conditioning coaches, dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists creates daily learning opportunities that build clinical competence and professional versatility.

## Credentialing Checklist for Military AT Candidates

For athletic trainers considering a military healthcare position, here is a practical checklist of what to have in order before applying:

1. **BOC certification** — Confirm active, unrestricted status and check your renewal date
2. **State licensure** — Identify the state where your target installation is located and begin the application if needed
3. **BLS/AED certification** — Ensure current certification from an AHA-approved provider
4. **Master's degree transcripts** — Have official transcripts available for verification
5. **Professional references** — Prepare references who can speak to your clinical competence
6. **Resume** — Updated to reflect military-relevant experience and credentials
7. **U.S. citizenship or permanent residency documentation** — Required for background investigation
8. **Clean background** — Be prepared for a thorough federal background check
