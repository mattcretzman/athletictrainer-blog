## What Is the H2F Program and Why Are Athletic Trainers in Demand?


H2F athletic trainers typically earn between $65,000 and $100,000+ annually in 2025, depending on experience, location, and contractor. Entry-level positions start around $60,000–$72,000, mid-career professionals earn $72,000–$85,000, and senior-level athletic trainers earn $85,000–$100,000+. Locality pay and OCONUS differentials can significantly increase total compensation.


If you are a certified athletic trainer evaluating the Holistic Health and Fitness system as a career path, compensation is likely one of the most important variables in your decision. This guide provides the most detailed H2F athletic trainer salary analysis available for 2025, covering every factor that influences what you can expect to earn.


### Overview of the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) System


The [Holistic Health and Fitness program](/blog/what-is-the-h2f-program) is the U.S. Army's primary investment in soldier readiness. Authorized under Army Regulation 7-22, H2F embeds interdisciplinary performance teams directly into brigade combat teams to address five domains of human performance: physical, nutritional, mental, spiritual, and sleep readiness.


The Army allocated approximately $345 million for the H2F program in the FY2024 budget — a clear signal of institutional commitment and long-term job stability for professionals entering this space.


### The Role of Athletic Trainers on H2F Performance Teams


Athletic trainers serve as core members of each [H2F performance team](/blog/h2f-performance-team-roles-explained). A typical team includes an athletic trainer, strength and conditioning coach, physical therapist, dietitian, occupational therapist, and cognitive performance specialist. As the athletic trainer, your responsibilities center on injury prevention, musculoskeletal assessment, rehabilitation, and return-to-duty protocols for service members.


This role differs fundamentally from traditional sports medicine settings. You are not managing gameday sidelines — you are embedded within a brigade, working alongside soldiers during physical training, field exercises, and deployment preparation cycles.


### H2F Program Expansion Timeline and Job Growth Projections for 2025


The Army's plan to embed H2F performance teams across all 40+ Active Duty brigade combat teams by end of FY2025 has dramatically expanded demand for qualified athletic trainers. That expansion — from roughly 10 brigades at program inception to full force-wide coverage — means hundreds of athletic trainer positions are being filled across installations nationwide and overseas.


This expansion has also intensified salary competition among defense contractors competing for the same pool of BOC-certified professionals, which has pushed compensation upward in recent hiring cycles.

## H2F Athletic Trainer Salary Ranges in 2025: A Complete Breakdown


Understanding H2F athletic trainer salary requires examining three tiers of experience. The figures below are based on aggregated job posting data from 2024–2025 contractor listings and represent base salary ranges before locality adjustments, benefits, or special allowances.


| Experience Level | Years Post-Certification | Salary Range (2025) |
|---|---|---|
| Entry-Level | 0–2 years | $60,000–$72,000 |
| Mid-Career | 3–7 years | $72,000–$85,000 |
| Senior / Lead AT | 8+ years | $85,000–$100,000+ |


### Entry-Level H2F Athletic Trainer Salary (0–2 Years Experience)


If you are newly BOC-certified or have up to two years of post-certification experience, you can expect an H2F athletic trainer salary in the range of $60,000 to $72,000 annually through contractor positions. This starting range already exceeds the national median for athletic trainers, which the Bureau of Labor Statistics reports at $56,420 (May 2023 data, the most recent available).


Entry-level H2F positions typically require a bachelor's degree at minimum, BOC certification, state licensure eligibility, CPR/AED certification, and the ability to obtain a Common Access Card (CAC).


### Mid-Career H2F Athletic Trainer Salary (3–7 Years Experience)


Athletic trainers with three to seven years of experience — particularly those with prior military healthcare or tactical performance backgrounds — typically command salaries between $72,000 and $85,000. At this level, holding a master's degree or additional certifications such as the CSCS can push you toward the higher end of this band.


Mid-career professionals are often positioned as the primary athletic trainer for their assigned brigade, carrying greater autonomy in program design and interdisciplinary coordination.


### Senior-Level and Lead H2F Athletic Trainer Salary (8+ Years Experience)


Senior athletic trainers and lead performance team members with eight or more years of experience can expect compensation of $85,000 to $100,000+. Lead roles may involve supervisory responsibilities across multiple teams or serve as the senior athletic trainer for an entire installation.


At this tier, a Doctor of Physical Therapy (DPT) degree, advanced specialty certifications, security clearance, and demonstrated military healthcare experience can elevate offers well above the $100,000 mark depending on location and contractor.


### H2F Salaries vs. National Athletic Trainer Averages (BLS Data Comparison)


| Metric | National Average (BLS) | H2F Contractor Range |
|---|---|---|
| Median Annual Salary | $56,420 | $75,000–$82,000 |
| Entry-Level (10th percentile) | $37,730 | $60,000–$72,000 |

As this comparison illustrates, H2F positions consistently exceed national averages across every experience tier. The floor for an H2F athletic trainer salary is roughly equivalent to the national median, and the ceiling extends well beyond what BLS reports for the profession overall.

### Fort Liberty (North Carolina) — Salary and Locality Pay


[Fort Liberty](/blog/athletic-trainer-jobs-at-military-installations) hosts one of the largest concentrations of H2F teams in the Army. The Raleigh-Durham locality pay area provides approximately a 20.22% adjustment for GS-scale positions. Contractor salaries here reflect the moderate cost of living typical of the Fayetteville area, with total compensation packages ranging from $68,000 to $92,000 depending on experience.


### Fort Campbell (Kentucky/Tennessee) — Salary and Locality Pay


Straddling the Kentucky-Tennessee border, Fort Campbell falls into either the Nashville locality pay area or the "Rest of US" category depending on the specific GS determination. Contractor salaries typically range from $65,000 to $88,000. The lower cost of living in the Clarksville area means your purchasing power at these salary levels is relatively strong compared to coastal installations.


### Joint Base Lewis-McChord (Washington) — Salary and Locality Pay


JBLM offers some of the highest H2F athletic trainer salaries in the continental United States. The Seattle-Tacoma locality adjustment of approximately 27.93% pushes GS-equivalent compensation significantly higher. Contractor positions here range from $72,000 to $98,000, reflecting the elevated cost of living in the Pacific Northwest.


### Fort Cavazos (Texas) — Salary and Locality Pay


Formerly Fort Hood, Fort Cavazos is located in the Killeen-Temple area of central Texas. While locality pay adjustments are more modest (approximately 19.45% under the "Rest of US" rate), the low cost of living in the region gives your salary considerable purchasing power. Expect contractor salaries from $65,000 to $88,000.


### Fort Stewart and Fort Moore (Georgia) — Salary and Locality Pay


Georgia hosts two major H2F installations. Fort Stewart near Hinesville applies the "Rest of US" locality rate (~17.91%), while Fort Moore near Columbus may receive a slightly higher adjustment. Contractor salaries at both installations typically range from $64,000 to $87,000. Georgia's affordable cost of living makes these positions financially attractive relative to their nominal salary figures.


### Fort Carson (Colorado) — Salary and Locality Pay


Fort Carson benefits from the Denver-Aurora locality pay adjustment of approximately 25.48%. Combined with strong demand for qualified athletic trainers in the Colorado Springs corridor, contractor salaries here range from $70,000 to $95,000. The outdoor recreation lifestyle and proximity to military-adjacent healthcare networks make this a popular assignment.


### OCONUS Locations: Germany, South Korea, Hawaii, and Alaska Pay Differentials


[OCONUS (outside the continental United States) assignments](/blog/oconus-military-healthcare-jobs) introduce additional compensation layers that can substantially increase your total earnings. These include:


- **COLA (Cost of Living Allowance):** Variable by location, designed to offset higher overseas expenses
- **Housing differentials:** May cover or subsidize housing costs in high-expense areas
- **Relocation bonuses:** Typically ranging from $3,000 to $10,000 depending on the contractor and assignment
- **Hardship pay or environmental differentials:** Available in certain duty stations


Hawaii and Alaska positions, while technically U.S. states, often carry COLA adjustments and special pay provisions. Germany and South Korea assignments may include per diem allowances during initial transition periods.

## Defense Contractor vs. Government (GS) Pay: How H2F Athletic Trainers Get Paid


Understanding the two primary compensation structures for H2F athletic trainers — defense contractor and General Schedule (GS) government pay — is essential for evaluating offers and planning your career trajectory.


### How Defense Contractor Compensation Works for H2F Positions


The majority of H2F athletic trainer positions are filled through defense contractors who hold prime or subcontract agreements with the Army. These contractors set their own salary bands, benefits packages, and hiring terms within the parameters of their government contract.


Contractor salaries for H2F athletic trainers typically range from $65,000 to $95,000 annually, with variation driven by the specific contractor, location, and your qualifications. Compensation is usually presented as a fixed annual salary rather than an hourly wage, though some contracts use hourly billing rates.


### Understanding GS Pay Grades (GS-9 Through GS-12) for Athletic Trainers


When H2F athletic trainer positions are filled through direct government hiring (less common but increasingly available), they typically fall under GS-9 to GS-12 pay bands.


| GS Grade | Step 1 (Base, 2025) | Step 10 (Base, 2025) | With 25% Locality |
|---|---|---|---|
| GS-9 | ~$52,000 | ~$67,600 | ~$65,000–$84,500 |
| GS-11 | ~$62,900 | ~$81,800 | ~$78,600–$102,250 |
| GS-12 | ~$75,400 | ~$98,000 | ~$94,250–$122,500 |


GS positions carry structured step increases and are subject to locality pay adjustments that can add 17% to 35%+ to your base salary depending on your duty station.


### Major H2F Contractors and Their Typical Compensation Packages


The primary defense contractors managing H2F performance teams include:


- **Accenture Federal Services** — The primary integrator for much of the H2F program, generally offering competitive salaries at or above market median
- **Leidos** — Known for comprehensive benefits packages and structured career progression
- **General Dynamics IT (GDIT)** — Competitive compensation with robust 401(k) matching
- **KBR** — Historically strong in OCONUS positions with enhanced relocation packages


Each contractor sets its own compensation structure. Salary offers for the same position at the same installation can vary by $5,000 to $12,000 between contractors, making it worthwhile to compare multiple offers when possible.


### Which Pay Structure Offers Better Long-Term Earning Potential?


The answer depends on your priorities. Contractor positions typically offer higher starting salaries and more flexibility in negotiation. GS positions offer greater long-term stability, predictable step increases, the Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS), and Thrift Savings Plan (TSP) contributions with matching.


For athletic trainers focused on maximizing short-to-medium-term income, contractor positions generally pay more. For those prioritizing retirement security and federal benefits over a 20+ year career, GS positions may provide superior lifetime earnings when factoring in pension and retirement benefits.

## Total Compensation Beyond Base Salary: Benefits and Perks


Base salary tells only part of the story. Your total compensation as an H2F athletic trainer includes benefits that can add 25% to 40% in value above your stated salary.


### Health Insurance and Retirement Benefits Through H2F Contractors


Most major H2F contractors provide comprehensive health insurance plans including medical, dental, and vision coverage. Retirement benefits typically include 401(k) plans with employer matching contributions, often in the range of 3% to 6% of salary. Some contractors also offer Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) and supplemental insurance options.


GS employees receive Federal Employees Health Benefits (FEHB) — one of the most comprehensive employer-sponsored health plans in the country — along with FERS retirement and TSP access.


### Paid Time Off, Federal Holidays, and Work-Life Balance


H2F contractor positions typically include 15 to 25 days of paid time off annually, scaling with tenure. Importantly, most H2F positions observe all 11 federal holidays. Some contractor positions also provide additional "down days" when the installation observes training holidays or block leave periods.


This benefit structure compares favorably to collegiate and clinical athletic trainer positions, where evening, weekend, and travel demands often reduce effective time off despite similar PTO allocations on paper.


### Relocation Allowances, Signing Bonuses, and Retention Incentives


As competition for qualified H2F athletic trainers has intensified with program expansion, contractors have increasingly offered financial incentives to attract talent:


- **Relocation allowances:** $3,000 to $10,000, particularly for OCONUS assignments or hard-to-fill installations
- **Signing bonuses:** Less common but appearing in high-demand locations, typically $2,000 to $5,000
- **Retention bonuses:** Offered at contract renewal points, particularly for senior athletic trainers with installation-specific experience


### COLA, Housing Differentials, and Per Diem for OCONUS Assignments


OCONUS compensation packages deserve dedicated attention. Beyond the base salary, you may receive:


- **Cost of Living Allowance (COLA):** Calculated based on duty station and updated quarterly; can add $5,000 to $20,000+ annually in high-cost locations like Germany or Hawaii
- **Living Quarters Allowance (LQA):** For GS employees stationed overseas, covering housing costs partially or fully
- **Per diem rates:** Applied during initial assignment periods or temporary duty stations, typically $150 to $300+ daily depending on location

## Factors That Increase Your H2F Athletic Trainer Salary


Not all H2F athletic trainers earn the same compensation at the same experience level. Several controllable factors can meaningfully increase your earning potential.


Factors that affect H2F athletic trainer salary include:


- **Advanced degrees (DPT, MS in Athletic Training, or related field)**
- **Specialty certifications (CSCS, PES, CES, FMS)**
- **Years of post-certification clinical experience**
- **Prior military service or military healthcare experience**
- **Active security clearance**
- **Geographic location and locality pay zone**
- **Specific contractor and contract vehicle**
- **Negotiation effectiveness during the hiring process**


### Advanced Degrees: How a DPT or Master's Degree Boosts Pay


A master's degree is increasingly preferred — and in some postings, required — for H2F athletic trainer positions. Holding a DPT or a master's in a performance-related field can add $5,000 to $12,000 to your starting offer compared to a bachelor's-only candidate at the same experience level. As the profession transitions toward graduate-level entry, this premium is likely to grow.


### Specialty Certifications: CSCS, PES, CES, and Their Salary Impact


The Certified Strength and Conditioning Specialist (CSCS) credential is the most commonly requested additional certification in H2F job postings. Holding a CSCS alongside your BOC certification signals versatility that contractors value, as H2F athletic trainers frequently collaborate on strength and conditioning programming. Additional certifications such as PES (Performance Enhancement Specialist) or CES (Corrective Exercise Specialist) further strengthen your profile and can support salary negotiations.


### Security Clearance and Prior Military Experience Premiums


An active security clearance — even at the Secret level — reduces onboarding time and administrative burden for contractors, making you a more attractive candidate. Some postings explicitly note a salary premium for candidates with existing clearances. Prior military service, particularly in combat arms or medical MOS fields, provides context and credibility that contractors and installation commanders value highly.


### Negotiation Strategies Specific to Defense Contractor Hiring


Defense contractor hiring processes differ from civilian healthcare hiring in important ways:

However, you can often negotiate within that band by emphasizing unique qualifications, asking about signing or relocation bonuses, and negotiating PTO accrual rates. Always ask about the full compensation package — not just base salary — before accepting or declining an offer.
</Callout>


Request the position's labor category and ask where the offered salary falls within that range. If the initial offer is at the lower end, substantiate your request for a higher rate with specific credentials, certifications, and relevant experience. Many candidates leave money on the table by not negotiating within the contractor's approved range.

## H2F Athletic Trainer Salary vs. Other Athletic Training Career Paths


Understanding how H2F compensation compares to other common career paths helps you make an informed decision about where to invest your professional energy.


### H2F vs. Collegiate Athletic Trainer Salary


Collegiate athletic trainer salaries vary dramatically by division level. NCAA Division I athletic trainers earn a median of approximately $50,000 to $60,000, with head athletic trainers at Power Five programs potentially earning $80,000 to $120,000+. However, the demanding schedule — including evenings, weekends, travel, and seasonal intensity — reduces the effective hourly rate significantly.


H2F positions offer comparable or higher base salaries with a more predictable schedule aligned to the military training day, typically 0600 to 1700.


### H2F vs. Professional Sports Athletic Trainer Salary


Professional sports athletic trainers in the NFL, NBA, MLB, and NHL earn $70,000 to $200,000+ depending on sport, league, and seniority. These figures are higher than most H2F salaries at the top end. However, professional sports positions are extremely limited in number, highly competitive, and require years of unpaid or low-paid internship and assistant positions. The path to a sustainable H2F salary is considerably more accessible.


### H2F vs. Clinical/Outpatient Rehabilitation Athletic Trainer Salary


Athletic trainers working in outpatient rehabilitation and clinical settings typically earn $45,000 to $65,000. Productivity-based compensation models in clinical settings can add variability and stress to earnings. H2F positions offer a clear salary premium of $10,000 to $30,000+ over most clinical athletic trainer roles while providing a mission-driven work environment.


### Why H2F May Offer the Best Salary-to-Lifestyle Ratio


When you factor in salary competitiveness, schedule predictability, federal holiday observance, benefits quality, and the absence of revenue-generation pressure, H2F positions present a compelling overall value proposition. The work is physically and intellectually demanding, but it follows a structured military schedule that provides more work-life balance than most comparable-salary positions in athletic training.

## How to Land a High-Paying H2F Athletic Trainer Position in 2025


Knowing the salary landscape is valuable only if you can position yourself to earn at the upper end of these ranges.


### Qualifications and Certifications Required for H2F Roles


Standard [H2F athletic trainer requirements](/blog/h2f-athletic-trainer-job-requirements) include:


- BOC certification (active and in good standing)
- State licensure eligibility for the installation's state
- Bachelor's degree minimum (master's degree increasingly preferred)
- Current CPR/AED certification
- Ability to obtain a Common Access Card (CAC) and pass a background investigation
- U.S. citizenship (required for most contractor positions)


Preferred qualifications that strengthen your candidacy and salary position include CSCS certification, a master's or doctoral degree, prior military or tactical athlete experience, and an existing security clearance.


### Where to Find H2F Job Postings (Contractor Portals and PSI Resources)


H2F positions are posted across multiple channels including contractor career portals (Accenture Federal Services, Leidos, GDIT, KBR), USAJobs.gov for GS positions, and specialized military healthcare recruitment platforms. Monitoring [current H2F job openings](/blog/h2f-job-openings-2025) through PSI provides access to curated listings with detailed compensation and requirements information.


### Tips for Maximizing Your Starting Salary Offer


1. **Obtain your CSCS before applying.** This single certification is the most consistently referenced differentiator in H2F job postings.
2. **Earn or complete a master's degree.** The pay premium is real and growing.
3. **Target high-locality-pay installations.** Applying to JBLM or Fort Carson instead of Fort Stewart can mean $5,000 to $10,000+ in additional annual compensation.
4. **Negotiate deliberately.** Ask for the labor category range, present your credentials in writing, and request the full compensation breakdown before responding to an offer.
5. **Consider OCONUS positions.** The additional allowances can make overseas assignments the highest-total-compensation option available.
