## Overview of Camp Lejeune: The East Coast's Premier Marine Corps Installation


If you are evaluating where to begin or advance a career in military healthcare, Camp Lejeune deserves a serious look. Spanning approximately 246 square miles along the coast of North Carolina, Camp Lejeune is one of the largest Marine Corps installations on the East Coast and one of the most operationally significant. For a certified athletic trainer, the combination of patient volume, mission diversity, and professional challenge here is difficult to match.


An athletic trainer at Camp Lejeune provides injury prevention, musculoskeletal assessment, rehabilitation, and return-to-duty care for active duty Marines across units including II Marine Expeditionary Force, Marine Corps Special Operations Command, and the School of Infantry-East. These professionals work within Semper Fit and Human Performance programs in close collaboration with Navy medical teams at the base's full-service hospital.


### Location, Size, and Mission of Camp Lejeune


Camp Lejeune sits adjacent to Jacksonville, North Carolina, roughly midway between Wilmington and the Outer Banks. The base's primary mission is to maintain trained and equipped Marine forces ready for expeditionary deployment. That mission drives everything you will encounter as a clinician here — the training tempo, the injury patterns, and the culture of readiness that defines daily operations.


The installation includes beaches used for amphibious assault training, dense inland training areas like Greater Sandy Run, multiple combat towns for urban warfare exercises, and airborne drop zones. Camp Geiger, a satellite installation, houses the School of Infantry-East. Nearby Stone Bay supports Marine Corps Special Operations Command training. Each of these environments generates distinct physical demands and, consequently, distinct clinical needs.


### Key Units Stationed at Camp Lejeune (II MEF, MARSOC, SOI-East)


Approximately 47,000 Marines and sailors are stationed at Camp Lejeune, a population that creates substantial demand for musculoskeletal and sports medicine care. The major units you should know include:


- **II Marine Expeditionary Force (II MEF):** The largest operational command on base, comprising infantry, artillery, logistics, and combat engineer battalions preparing for global deployment.
- **Marine Corps Special Operations Command (MARSOC):** Elite operators based at Camp Lejeune and Stone Bay who require dedicated human performance support and advanced rehabilitation services.
- **School of Infantry-East (SOI-E):** Located at Camp Geiger, SOI-E trains thousands of newly minted Marines each year in infantry and combat-support military occupational specialties, producing a high volume of training-related injuries.
- **Wounded Warrior Battalion-East (WWBn-E):** Headquartered at Camp Lejeune, this unit provides recovery care coordination for Marines with serious injuries or illnesses.


### Why Camp Lejeune Is a High-Demand Location for Athletic Trainers


The sheer scale of the service member population, combined with the physical intensity of Marine Corps training, means that Camp Lejeune consistently generates openings for athletic trainers through both government civilian (GS) positions and government contractor roles. The base supports multiple human performance and injury prevention programs simultaneously, creating a variety of clinical settings where your skills are directly applied to force readiness.

## The Athletic Trainer Role at Camp Lejeune: Daily Responsibilities


Your day-to-day work as an athletic trainer at Camp Lejeune will look different from a traditional sports medicine setting, but the clinical foundations remain the same. The population is younger, highly motivated, and engaged in physical activity that often exceeds the demands of competitive athletics.


### Injury Prevention and Screening for Active Duty Marines


Much of your value at Camp Lejeune lies in proactive care. You may conduct movement screens, Functional Movement Screens (FMS), or unit-specific physical readiness assessments to identify service members at elevated risk for musculoskeletal injury. Before major training events — a 20-mile forced march, an amphibious landing exercise, a combatives qualification — you may work with unit leadership to implement prehabilitation protocols that reduce time-loss injuries.


### Rehabilitation and Return-to-Duty Protocols


When injuries occur, your role centers on getting Marines back to full duty as safely and efficiently as possible. Return-to-duty is the standard metric here, not return-to-play. You will manage acute and subacute musculoskeletal conditions, design progressive rehabilitation programs, and make clinical decisions about when a service member is ready to resume full training. Your assessments carry real weight in operational decisions.


### Supporting Human Performance Optimization Programs


Athletic trainers embedded in Human Performance programs at Camp Lejeune work alongside strength and conditioning specialists, sports dietitians, mental performance coaches, and sports psychologists. Your contribution focuses on the musculoskeletal health side of the performance equation — ensuring that service members can train at the volumes and intensities their mission demands without breaking down.


### Common Injuries and Conditions You'll Treat at a Marine Corps Base


The injury profile at a Marine Corps installation reflects the training environment. Conditions you will see regularly include:


- **Lower extremity stress fractures** from high-volume running and ruck marching
- **Ankle sprains and instability** related to uneven terrain and obstacle course training
- **Knee injuries** including patellofemoral syndrome, meniscal tears, and ACL sprains from combatives and dismounted operations
- **Lumbar spine overuse injuries** from heavy load carriage (packs frequently exceed 80 pounds)
- **Shoulder injuries** including labral pathology and rotator cuff strains from overhead lifting, rope climbs, and obstacle negotiation
- **Exertional injuries** such as heat-related illness and exertional rhabdomyolysis, particularly during North Carolina's humid summer months
- **Plantar fasciitis and Achilles tendinopathy** from high running mileage in boots
- **Concussions** from combatives, breaching operations, and blast exposure training


<Callout type="tip">
Familiarize yourself with the Marine Corps Physical Fitness Test (PFT) and Combat Fitness Test (CFT) standards before arriving at Camp Lejeune. Understanding the specific movements and thresholds Marines must achieve helps you design rehabilitation programs with meaningful return-to-duty benchmarks.
</Callout>

## Programs and Teams You May Be Embedded With


The specific program you join will shape your daily schedule, your patient population, and the clinical challenges you face. Camp Lejeune supports several distinct pathways for athletic trainers.


### Semper Fit and Force Fitness Programs


The Semper Fit program is the Marine Corps' comprehensive health and fitness initiative, and it employs athletic trainers in injury prevention and rehabilitation roles across the installation. Force Fitness Instructors (FFIs) assigned to individual units often serve as your primary point of contact for identifying service members who need clinical attention. Your work within Semper Fit may involve managing a clinic-based caseload, conducting field-side coverage during training events, or running injury prevention education for entire battalions.


### MARSOC Human Performance Teams


If you are drawn to working with special operations forces, the MARSOC Human Performance Program at Camp Lejeune and Stone Bay offers one of the most clinically demanding and professionally rewarding environments in military athletic training. You will be embedded with Marine Raiders and Critical Skills Operators, providing daily hands-on care within a fully integrated human performance team. The physical demands on this population are extreme, and the expectation is that you deliver advanced clinical reasoning and evidence-based interventions.


### Wounded Warrior Battalion-East Rehabilitation Support


The Wounded Warrior Battalion-East serves Marines and sailors recovering from serious injuries, illnesses, and wounds. Athletic trainers supporting this program contribute to long-term rehabilitation plans, adaptive reconditioning, and transition-related fitness goals. The work requires patience, clinical creativity, and the ability to collaborate across a broad interdisciplinary team that may include occupational therapists, prosthetists, and behavioral health specialists.


### School of Infantry-East Injury Prevention Initiatives


SOI-East at Camp Geiger processes thousands of Marines through infantry and combat-support training pipelines each year. The attrition and injury rates in these courses create a compelling case for athletic trainer involvement. You may screen incoming students, provide sideline care during high-risk training evolutions, and track injury data to inform programmatic changes that reduce musculoskeletal casualties.

## Working with Navy Medicine and Interdisciplinary Teams


One of the defining features of clinical practice at Camp Lejeune is the depth of your interdisciplinary network. You are not working in isolation.


### Collaboration with Naval Medical Center Camp Lejeune


Naval Medical Center Camp Lejeune is a full-service military hospital located on the installation. When a service member in your care needs advanced imaging, surgical consultation, or specialist referral, this facility is your primary resource. Building strong referral relationships with orthopedic surgeons, sports medicine physicians, and radiologists at the medical center is essential to efficient patient management.


### Your Role Alongside Physical Therapists, Physicians, and Strength Coaches


In most Camp Lejeune programs, athletic trainers work within a team that includes physical therapists, primary care physicians or sports medicine doctors, strength and conditioning coaches, and — in some settings — dietitians and mental performance specialists. Your scope of practice and daily responsibilities will depend on your specific program, but in general, you serve as the first point of musculoskeletal contact for service members and coordinate care along the continuum from acute injury through return to full duty.


### Credentialing and Privileging Process for Athletic Trainers


Working within or alongside Navy Medicine requires completing a credentialing and privileging process. This typically involves submitting your Board of Certification (BOC) credential, state licensure documentation, continuing education records, and malpractice history. The process can take several weeks to several months, so beginning the paperwork early — ideally before your start date — is advisable. Your employer, whether a government agency or a contractor like PSI, will guide you through the specific requirements.


<Callout type="tip">
Start gathering your credentialing documents as soon as you accept a position. Having your BOC certification verification, state license, CPR card, transcripts, and immunization records organized in advance can prevent delays in your ability to begin treating patients.
</Callout>

## Employment Logistics: How to Get Hired as an Athletic Trainer at Camp Lejeune


Understanding the employment landscape is critical to making a well-informed decision. Athletic trainer positions at Camp Lejeune fall into two primary categories, each with distinct advantages.


### GS Civilian Positions vs. Government Contractor Roles


| Factor | GS Civilian (Federal Employee) | Government Contractor |
|---|---|---|
| **Employer** | Department of Defense / Navy | Private company (e.g., PSI) |
| **Pay Structure** | GS pay scale with locality adjustment | Contract-defined salary |
| **Typical Grade** | GS-11 to GS-12 | N/A (varies by contract) |
| **Benefits** | Federal benefits (FEHB, FERS, TSP) | Employer-provided benefits |
| **Job Security** | High (federal employment protections) | Contract-dependent |
| **Hiring Speed** | Slower (USAJobs process) | Generally faster |
| **Flexibility** | Less (federal HR processes) | More (direct hire) |
| **Relocation Assistance** | Sometimes available | Varies by employer |


Both pathways lead to meaningful clinical work. The right choice depends on your career goals, timeline, and personal priorities.


### Salary Expectations and GS Pay Scale for Jacksonville, NC


For GS civilian positions, athletic trainers at Camp Lejeune typically fall within the GS-11 to GS-12 range. Jacksonville, NC, falls under the "Rest of US" locality pay area, which adds approximately 17.06% to base pay (2024 figures). That translates to:


- **GS-11, Step 1:** Approximately $82,800 annually with locality pay
- **GS-12, Step 1:** Approximately $99,200 annually with locality pay


Contractor salaries vary based on the specific contract and employer but generally fall within a comparable range. Some contractor positions may offer additional allowances or benefits such as relocation stipends.


### Required Certifications and Clearances


At minimum, you will need:


- **BOC Certification** (current and in good standing)
- **State licensure** from North Carolina or eligibility to obtain it
- **BLS/CPR certification**
- **Background investigation** (typically a National Agency Check with Inquiries, or NACI, for most positions; some MARSOC-related roles may require a higher-level security clearance)


### How to Apply Through USAJobs and Contract Recruiters


For GS positions, your application goes through USAJobs.gov. These postings require careful attention to the specific qualifications, KSAs (Knowledge, Skills, and Abilities), and documentation requirements. Incomplete applications are routinely screened out.


For contractor positions, companies like PSI recruit directly. PSI maintains a career portal where you can view current openings at Camp Lejeune and other military installations, submit your credentials, and speak with a recruiter who understands the specific clinical requirements of each position.

## Living at or Near Camp Lejeune: Quality of Life for Athletic Trainers


Salary is only one part of the equation. Where and how you live matters just as much, and Camp Lejeune's location in coastal North Carolina offers a lifestyle that balances affordability with access to beaches, outdoor recreation, and a close-knit military community.


### On-Base Housing vs. Off-Base Communities


As a civilian employee or contractor, you will not have access to on-base military family housing, but you will have a range of off-base options:


- **Jacksonville, NC:** The largest nearby city, offering the widest selection of apartments, townhomes, and single-family homes. Most rentals and purchases are within a 10- to 20-minute commute of base gates.
- **Sneads Ferry:** A quieter coastal community south of base, popular with those who want proximity to Topsail Island beaches.
- **Swansboro:** A small, historic waterfront town east of base, known for its charm and slightly slower pace.
- **Hubert:** A rural community between Jacksonville and Swansboro, offering larger properties and a more secluded feel.


### Cost of Living in Coastal North Carolina


Jacksonville, NC, is affordable relative to many military installation areas. As of 2024, the median home price in the area ranges from approximately $250,000 to $280,000. Average rent for a two-bedroom apartment runs between $1,100 and $1,400 per month. Groceries, utilities, and transportation costs are generally at or below the national average. When paired with a GS-11 or GS-12 salary, or a competitive contractor wage, this cost of living allows for a comfortable standard of living.


### Base Amenities, Recreation, and Beach Access


Camp Lejeune offers on-base amenities available to civilian employees and contractors, including fitness centers, dining facilities, and exchange shopping. Onslow Beach, located on the base, provides direct ocean access. Off base, the Crystal Coast and Topsail Island are within easy driving distance, offering surfing, fishing, kayaking, and some of the best beaches in the Southeast.


### Family Life and Schools in the Camp Lejeune Area


Onslow County operates the public school system serving most families in the area. Department of Defense Education Activity (DoDEA) schools are also available on base for military families. The community is deeply connected to the military, which means local services, healthcare, and social infrastructure are accustomed to the rhythms and realities of military life.

## What Makes Camp Lejeune Unique Compared to Other Military Athletic Trainer Positions


Not all military installations are created equal from a clinical standpoint. Camp Lejeune presents a distinctive combination of factors that shape both the challenge and the reward of working here.


### High Operational Tempo and Its Impact on Your Caseload


II MEF units maintain a demanding deployment and training cycle. The operational tempo means your patient population is constantly preparing for, executing, or recovering from physically intense training events. Caseloads tend to be high, and the urgency of returning Marines to duty status adds a layer of clinical pressure that sharpens decision-making.


### Amphibious and Expeditionary Training Environments


Few installations expose athletic trainers to the range of training environments found at Camp Lejeune. Amphibious landing exercises, jungle and swamp lane navigation, long-range desert-simulated patrols, and urban warfare drills each produce unique biomechanical stresses. Your clinical reasoning will broaden as you learn to anticipate and manage injuries tied to these varied operational contexts.


### Career Growth and Professional Development Opportunities


The concentration of clinical programs, Navy Medicine assets, and performance teams at Camp Lejeune creates opportunities for professional growth that extend well beyond your initial role. Many athletic trainers use their time at Camp Lejeune to earn tactical-specific certifications, present at military sports medicine conferences, contribute to research on injury prevention in military populations, or transition into leadership roles within their program.


### Networking with the Largest Concentration of Marines on the East Coast


Working at Camp Lejeune places you within the largest community of Marines on the Eastern Seaboard. The professional relationships you build — with military physicians, special operations human performance directors, physical therapists, and fellow athletic trainers — form a network that can support your career for years to come.

## Tips for Thriving as an Athletic Trainer at Camp Lejeune


Arriving prepared is the difference between a steep learning curve and a smooth integration into your new role.


### Understanding Marine Corps Culture and Earning Trust


Marines value competence, directness, and physical toughness. Earning trust with your patient population requires demonstrating clinical skill quickly and consistently. Learn the rank structure. Use proper terminology. Show up to field training events when you say you will. Your credibility is built one interaction at a time.


### Adapting to Outdoor Training in North Carolina's Climate


Camp Lejeune's climate features hot, humid summers with average highs around 90°F and mild winters. Outdoor training occurs year-round. This means you will encounter heat-related conditions frequently during summer months and should be prepared to provide care in field environments that lack climate control. Hydration strategies, heat injury recognition, and cold-weather protocols for winter night operations should all be in your toolkit.


### Building Relationships with Unit Leadership


Your effectiveness depends significantly on your relationships with company commanders, first sergeants, and platoon leaders. When unit leadership trusts your clinical judgment, they are more likely to incorporate your injury prevention recommendations into training plans and to refer service members to you before minor issues become major ones.


### Continuing Education and Tactical Athletic Trainer Specialization


The Board of Certification's continuing education requirements align well with the professional development opportunities available at Camp Lejeune. Consider pursuing the Tactical Strength and Conditioning Facilitator (TSAC-F) credential from the NSCA, attending the Military Sports Medicine Conference, or engaging with the Consortium for Health and Military Performance (CHAMP) resources. These investments deepen your expertise and increase your value within the military healthcare ecosystem.


<Callout type="tip">
If you have never worked in a military setting before, consider shadowing a military athletic trainer or attending an orientation event before your first day. Understanding how to navigate base access, military time, and the chain of command will help you focus on clinical care from the start rather than logistical learning curves.
</Callout>
