# Fort Cavazos Athletic Trainer Jobs: Living and Working in Central Texas


Fort Cavazos — formerly Fort Hood — is the largest active-duty armored military installation in the world, home to more than 45,000 soldiers and a growing network of healthcare and performance optimization professionals. Athletic trainer jobs at Fort Cavazos are available primarily through the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program and clinical settings such as Darnall Army Medical Center. Positions are offered as GS-9 to GS-11 government civilian roles or through defense contractors, and they require BOC certification along with Texas state licensure. For certified athletic trainers seeking meaningful, stable careers in military healthcare, Central Texas offers a compelling combination of professional opportunity, affordability, and quality of life.

## Why Fort Cavazos Is a Leading Destination for Athletic Trainers


### The Name Change: From Fort Hood to Fort Cavazos


On May 9, 2023, Fort Hood was officially redesignated as Fort Cavazos, honoring General Richard E. Cavazos — the first Hispanic four-star general in U.S. Army history. If you have been searching for "Fort Hood athletic trainer jobs," those listings now appear under the Fort Cavazos name. Both terms may still surface in job databases during the transition period, so searching for either designation is advisable when conducting your job research.


### Fort Cavazos by the Numbers


Spanning roughly 214,000 acres of Central Texas terrain, Fort Cavazos is home to III Corps headquarters, the 1st Cavalry Division, elements of the 1st Armored Division, and numerous supporting units. The installation's sheer scale — and the physical demands placed on tens of thousands of soldiers conducting armored and combined-arms training — creates a sustained need for healthcare professionals who specialize in musculoskeletal injury prevention, assessment, and rehabilitation.


### Growing Demand for Athletic Trainers on Military Installations


The Department of Defense has recognized that keeping service members physically ready requires more than reactive medical care. The Army's ongoing investment in performance optimization, particularly through the H2F system, has expanded the role of certified athletic trainers from a niche presence to an embedded, essential component of brigade-level healthcare. Fort Cavazos, with its concentration of Brigade Combat Teams, is one of the highest-demand installations for these positions in the entire Army.

## Athletic Trainer Job Opportunities at Fort Cavazos


### H2F (Holistic Health and Fitness) Program Positions


The Holistic Health and Fitness program represents the Army's most significant shift in how it approaches soldier readiness. Each H2F performance team is embedded within a Brigade Combat Team and typically includes one to two athletic trainers alongside strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, registered dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists.


As an athletic trainer within H2F at Fort Cavazos, your role centers on:


- **Injury prevention screening and movement assessments** for soldiers preparing for training cycles and deployments
- **On-field injury evaluation and triage** during physical training, field exercises, and combatives
- **Rehabilitation programming** in coordination with physical therapists and orthopedic providers
- **Education and outreach** to unit leaders on injury trends, load management, and recovery strategies
- **Data collection and documentation** in support of population health analytics and readiness metrics


Because Fort Cavazos supports multiple Brigade Combat Teams, the installation may have openings for several athletic trainers at any given time — a concentration of opportunity that smaller posts cannot match.


### Darnall Army Medical Center and Clinic-Based Roles


Darnall Army Medical Center is the primary military medical facility on Fort Cavazos, offering comprehensive inpatient and outpatient services. Athletic trainers may find positions within the orthopedic, sports medicine, or rehabilitation departments, where the focus shifts toward clinical evaluation, post-surgical rehabilitation, and collaborative care with physicians and surgeons.


Clinic-based roles often provide a more traditional medical setting, regular hours, and access to advanced diagnostic equipment. These positions may appeal to athletic trainers who prefer a clinical practice model over the embedded, operational tempo of H2F assignments.


### Government Civilian (GS) vs. Defense Contractor Positions


Athletic trainer jobs at Fort Cavazos generally fall into two employment categories:


**Government Civilian (GS) Positions**
- Hired through USAJobs.gov under the Department of the Army
- Classified under GS-9 to GS-11 pay grades, depending on experience and qualifications
- Include federal benefits: FEHB health insurance, FERS retirement, Thrift Savings Plan, paid leave
- Offer long-term job stability and structured career progression


**Defense Contractor Positions**
- Hired through private companies contracted by the Army to staff H2F and medical programs
- Salaries are often competitive with or slightly above GS equivalents
- Benefits vary by employer but typically include health insurance, retirement plans, and PTO
- May offer faster hiring timelines and more flexibility in assignment locations


Both pathways require the same clinical credentials. Your choice between them often comes down to how you prioritize benefits, career trajectory, and hiring speed.


### Typical Job Responsibilities and Daily Workflow


A typical day for an athletic trainer at Fort Cavazos may begin before 0600, aligning with the Army's physical training schedule. Morning hours often involve observing and supporting physical readiness training, conducting movement screens, or providing acute injury assessment on the training fields. Mid-day hours shift toward treatment and rehabilitation sessions, interdisciplinary team meetings, and documentation. Afternoons may include educational briefings for unit leaders, follow-up evaluations, or professional development activities.


The tempo varies. During pre-deployment training cycles, the pace increases substantially. During post-deployment recovery phases, the emphasis shifts toward long-term rehabilitation and injury prevention programming.

## Qualifications and Credentialing Requirements


### BOC Certification and Education Requirements


All athletic trainer positions at Fort Cavazos require current certification from the Board of Certification (BOC). This means you must hold a degree from a CAATE-accredited athletic training program — now at the master's level for new graduates — and maintain your certification through continuing education.


### Texas State Athletic Trainer Licensure (TDLR)


Texas requires athletic trainers to hold a license issued by the Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR). If you are relocating from another state, you should initiate the Texas licensure application process early, as it can take several weeks. Requirements include proof of BOC certification, a completed application, applicable fees, and a background check.


<Callout type="tip">
Begin your Texas TDLR licensure application as soon as you receive a conditional job offer. Processing times can vary, and having your license in hand accelerates the onboarding timeline. Check the TDLR website for the most current application requirements and fees.
</Callout>


### Military Installation Credentialing Process


Working at a military installation requires an additional layer of credentialing beyond your state license and national certification. You will need to be credentialed through the installation's medical authority, which involves verification of your education, certifications, licensure, work history, and malpractice history. This process is standard across Department of Defense facilities and is separate from any security clearance requirements.


### Security Clearance and Background Check Considerations


Most athletic trainer positions at Fort Cavazos require at minimum a National Agency Check with Inquiries (NACI) or a Tier 1 background investigation for base access. Some positions — particularly those embedded with operational units — may require a higher level of clearance. The hiring agency or contractor will initiate and sponsor this process; you do not need to hold a clearance before applying.

## Living in Central Texas: A Complete Guide


### Killeen: The Closest Community to Fort Cavazos


Killeen is the largest city adjacent to Fort Cavazos and the most common choice for installation employees seeking a short commute. With a population of approximately 155,000, Killeen offers a full range of shopping, dining, schools, and healthcare services. The city's economy is closely tied to the military installation, which means a community that understands and supports military-adjacent professionals.


Median home prices in Killeen hover around $230,000–$260,000, making homeownership accessible on an athletic trainer's salary. Rental options are also abundant, with one- and two-bedroom apartments typically ranging from $900 to $1,400 per month.


### Harker Heights, Copperas Cove, and Other Nearby Towns


- **Harker Heights** sits just east of Killeen and offers a somewhat more suburban feel with newer developments, well-regarded schools, and a slightly higher price point than central Killeen.
- **Copperas Cove** is located to the west of the installation and appeals to those seeking a quieter, small-town atmosphere. Commute times to Fort Cavazos are typically 20–30 minutes.
- **Nolanville** and **Gatesville** provide rural living options for those who prefer acreage and open space.


### Temple and Belton: Living Outside the Military Corridor


Temple (population ~82,000) and Belton (~25,000) sit approximately 30–40 minutes from Fort Cavazos along the I-35 corridor. These communities offer a more distinct identity from the military installation, with Temple's strong healthcare economy (anchored by Baylor Scott & White Medical Center) and Belton's proximity to the University of Mary Hardin-Baylor providing cultural and professional diversity.


If your spouse is seeking employment outside the defense sector, Temple and Belton offer a broader range of opportunities in healthcare, education, retail, and professional services.


### Cost of Living: Housing, Groceries, and Everyday Expenses


The Killeen-Temple-Fort Cavazos metro area consistently ranks 15–20% below the national cost of living average. Key categories:


- **Housing:** Significantly below national median prices for both purchase and rent
- **Groceries:** Roughly at or slightly below national averages, with access to commissary shopping on base
- **Transportation:** Lower than national averages due to shorter commutes and lower fuel costs
- **Healthcare:** Generally in line with national averages; TRICARE-adjacent benefits available for federal employees


### The Texas Financial Advantage


Texas levies no state income tax. For an athletic trainer earning $65,000 annually, this can mean $3,000–$5,000 more in take-home pay compared to states with moderate income tax rates. Combined with the region's lower cost of living, your salary stretches further in Central Texas than in many other locations with athletic trainer positions on military installations.

## Climate, Lifestyle, and Recreation in Central Texas


### Weather and Climate: What to Expect Year-Round


Central Texas has a subtropical climate characterized by hot summers and mild winters. Average high temperatures reach 97°F in July and August, while January lows hover around 37°F. Spring and fall offer the most comfortable conditions for outdoor activity. If you are relocating from a northern climate, be prepared for an extended warm season — and know that many outdoor training activities on the installation begin early in the morning to mitigate heat exposure.


### Outdoor Recreation


Central Texas offers more natural beauty than many newcomers expect:


- **Belton Lake and Stillhouse Hollow Lake** provide opportunities for boating, fishing, kayaking, and lakeside camping, all within 20–30 minutes of the installation
- **Dana Peak Park** and **Chalk Ridge Falls** are popular hiking and nature destinations
- **Fort Cavazos itself** encompasses vast areas of open terrain, with hunting and fishing programs available to installation employees
- **Mother Neff State Park** — the first state park in Texas — is a short drive from the installation


### Dining, Entertainment, and Cultural Attractions


While the Killeen-Temple corridor may not offer the same cultural density as Austin or San Antonio, the area has a growing dining scene, local breweries, movie theaters, and community events. The Killeen Civic and Conference Center and Vive Les Arts Theatre provide local entertainment options.


### Proximity to Major Cities


One of Central Texas's distinct advantages is access to multiple major metropolitan areas:


- **Austin:** ~60 miles south (approximately 1 hour). Live music, dining, tech industry, University of Texas
- **Waco:** ~50 miles north (approximately 45 minutes). Magnolia Market, Baylor University, Cameron Park
- **San Antonio:** ~150 miles south (approximately 2.5 hours). River Walk, military installations (JBSA), cultural attractions
- **Dallas-Fort Worth:** ~190 miles north (approximately 3 hours). Professional sports, major airports, corporate headquarters

## Working on a Military Installation: What Athletic Trainers Should Know


### Understanding Military Culture and Communication


Working alongside service members requires an understanding of military structure, customs, and communication norms. You are not expected to have military experience, but a willingness to learn ranks, unit organization, and the operational context of your patients' physical demands is essential. Your effectiveness as an athletic trainer in this environment depends as much on cultural competence as clinical skill.


<Callout type="tip">
Familiarize yourself with basic military rank structures, common acronyms, and the Army Physical Fitness Test (ACFT) standards before your first day. Understanding what soldiers are training for — and what physical standards they must meet — allows you to provide more relevant and respected clinical care.
</Callout>


### Collaborating with Military Medical Professionals


Athletic trainers at Fort Cavazos work within interdisciplinary teams that may include Army physicians, physical therapists, occupational therapists, physician assistants, and medics. In H2F settings, collaboration extends to strength coaches, dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists. The ability to communicate clearly across disciplines, maintain thorough documentation, and respect the scope of each team member's practice is critical.


### Career Growth and Professional Development


Military installations generally support continuing education and professional development. Government civilian employees may have access to tuition assistance programs, funded conference attendance, and opportunities to pursue additional certifications. Contractor employees often receive professional development stipends or reimbursement as part of their benefits packages.


Fort Cavazos's size also means opportunities for upward mobility — whether moving into a senior athletic trainer role, a program coordinator position, or a supervisory capacity within H2F or the medical center.


### Benefits of Federal Employment and Contractor Packages


Federal benefits for GS employees are among the most comprehensive in the healthcare sector:


- **Federal Employees Health Benefits (FEHB):** Wide range of plan options
- **Federal Employees Retirement System (FERS):** Defined benefit pension, Social Security, and TSP (with agency match)
- **Paid leave:** 13–26 days of annual leave per year (based on tenure), 13 days of sick leave, 11 federal holidays
- **Life insurance and disability coverage**


Contractor benefits vary but are increasingly competitive, particularly through established defense healthcare staffing organizations.

## How to Apply for Athletic Trainer Jobs at Fort Cavazos


### Finding Positions on USAJobs and Contractor Job Boards


Government civilian athletic trainer positions are posted on [USAJobs.gov](https://www.usajobs.gov). Search using keywords such as "athletic trainer," "health technician," or "H2F" along with the Fort Cavazos location. Be aware that federal job postings may use different occupational series codes, so broadening your search terms can reveal positions that might otherwise be missed.


Contractor positions are listed on the career pages of defense healthcare staffing companies and on industry job boards. New positions tend to post in cycles aligned with contract awards and renewals.


### Applying Through PSI


PSI maintains current listings for athletic trainer positions at Fort Cavazos and other military installations. Working with PSI can streamline your search by matching your credentials and preferences to open positions, guiding you through the credentialing process, and providing insight into installation-specific requirements that may not be apparent from a job listing alone.


### Resume Tips for Military Athletic Trainer Positions


Your resume for a military athletic trainer position should emphasize:


- **Clinical credentials:** BOC certification, state licensure (or eligibility), CPR/AED certification
- **Relevant experience:** Musculoskeletal injury evaluation, rehabilitation, emergency care, population health
- **Interdisciplinary collaboration:** Experience working within team-based care models
- **Quantifiable outcomes:** Injury reduction rates, patient volumes, program development achievements
- **Technology proficiency:** Electronic health records (AHLTA/MHS GENESIS), data tracking systems


For USAJobs applications, follow the federal resume format — more detailed than a private sector resume — and address every qualification listed in the job announcement.


### Interview Preparation


Interviews for military athletic trainer positions often focus on clinical scenarios, your understanding of the military healthcare environment, and your ability to function within a team. Be prepared to discuss how you would manage high patient volumes, adapt evidence-based protocols to a non-traditional setting, and communicate with non-medical leadership about injury trends and readiness impacts.
