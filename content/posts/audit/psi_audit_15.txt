## Fort Liberty (Formerly Fort Bragg): Why This Installation Is a Top Destination for Athletic Trainers


Fort Liberty (formerly Fort Bragg), located in Fayetteville, North Carolina, employs certified athletic trainers through the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program and special operations human performance teams. Positions are available as government service (GS) roles or through private contractors like PSI, with salaries typically ranging from $55,000 to $85,000 depending on the role, unit assignment, and experience level.


For athletic trainers considering a move into military healthcare, Fort Liberty represents one of the most dynamic and consequential practice environments in the country. The installation's unique combination of elite units, high operational tempo, and investment in human performance creates a professional setting unlike any civilian sports medicine clinic.


### The Name Change: Fort Bragg to Fort Liberty — What It Means for Your Job Search


On June 2, 2023, Fort Bragg was officially redesignated as Fort Liberty as part of the Department of Defense's congressionally mandated naming commission. While the name change is well established, its practical impact on your job search is worth understanding.


<Callout type="tip">
Search for openings under both "Fort Liberty" and "Fort Bragg." Many job boards, contractor databases, and even some USAJobs listings still reference the former name. Running parallel searches ensures you capture every available posting.
</Callout>


Older position descriptions, contracts, and facility references may still use the Fort Bragg designation. This is especially common on third-party job aggregators that pull from cached listings. Do not assume a "Fort Bragg" listing is outdated — verify the posting date and confirm directly with the hiring organization.


### Fort Liberty by the Numbers: Size, Units, and Mission Overview


Fort Liberty is the largest military installation by population in the United States, home to approximately 57,000 active-duty service members and more than 11,000 civilian employees. The installation spans roughly 251 square miles and serves as the headquarters for several of the Army's most high-profile commands:


- **U.S. Army Special Operations Command (USASOC):** The command and control headquarters for all Army special operations forces, including Special Forces (Green Berets), the 75th Ranger Regiment, and Civil Affairs and Psychological Operations units.
- **82nd Airborne Division:** America's Guard of Honor, maintaining the ability to deploy anywhere in the world within 18 hours.
- **Joint Special Operations Command (JSOC):** One of the most operationally significant commands in the U.S. military, with missions that often require the highest security clearances.
- **U.S. Army Forces Command (FORSCOM):** The largest Army command, responsible for training and readiness of conventional forces.


Each of these commands has distinct human performance needs, and each creates demand for athletic trainers with specialized skill sets.


### Why the Demand for Athletic Trainers at Fort Liberty Is Growing


Two major institutional shifts have driven significant growth in athletic trainer jobs at Fort Liberty over the past several years.


First, the Army's H2F System, established in 2020 under Field Manual 7-22, formalized the integration of certified athletic trainers into brigade-level performance teams. Each H2F team typically includes one to two athletic trainers per brigade combat team, and Fort Liberty's concentration of brigades means multiple concurrent openings are common.


Second, USASOC and its subordinate units have long operated human performance programs — including the Tactical Athlete Performance Program (TA2P) — that embed athletic trainers directly with operational units. These programs predate H2F and continue to expand as the Army refines its approach to human performance optimization.


The result is a growing and diversified demand for athletic trainers at a single installation, spanning both conventional and special operations forces.


## Types of Athletic Trainer Positions Available at Fort Liberty


Understanding the different employment structures at Fort Liberty is essential before you apply. Not all positions carry the same responsibilities, compensation, or security requirements.


### H2F (Holistic Health and Fitness) Program Positions


The H2F program represents the largest source of new athletic trainer positions across the Army, and Fort Liberty is one of its flagship implementation sites. H2F performance teams are embedded at the brigade level and function as multidisciplinary units that include athletic trainers, strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, occupational therapists, dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists.


As an athletic trainer within an H2F team, your role centers on musculoskeletal injury prevention, acute injury assessment, rehabilitation, and return-to-duty decision-making. You work directly with company- and battalion-level formations during physical readiness training, field exercises, and daily sick call.


H2F positions at Fort Liberty are typically filled through private military contractors who hold the Army's H2F service contract. PSI is among the organizations that support these staffing requirements.


### Special Operations and USASOC Human Performance Roles


Athletic trainer positions supporting special operations units at Fort Liberty carry a distinct profile. These roles often fall under human performance optimization programs that have operated independently of H2F and may include embedded support for Special Forces groups, Ranger battalions, or other USASOC elements.


The patient population in these roles consists of highly trained tactical athletes who maintain demanding physical standards and face unique injury profiles associated with airborne operations, close-quarters combat training, and prolonged field operations. Athletic trainers in these settings are expected to operate with a high degree of autonomy and clinical judgment.


These positions are more likely to require a Secret or Top Secret security clearance due to the sensitive nature of the units and their operational schedules.

These roles tend to be more clinically oriented, focusing on post-surgical rehabilitation, outpatient musculoskeletal care, and referral-based patient management rather than embedded unit support.


While fewer in number, Womack-based positions offer a more traditional clinical environment and may appeal to athletic trainers who prefer a hospital or clinic setting over fieldwork.

## Qualifications and Requirements You Need Before Applying


Fort Liberty positions carry specific credentialing and eligibility requirements. Meeting these before you apply streamlines the hiring process and demonstrates readiness to prospective employers.


### Education and BOC Certification Requirements


All athletic trainer positions at Fort Liberty require current certification from the Board of Certification (BOC). This is non-negotiable for both GS and contractor roles.


A bachelor's degree from a CAATE-accredited athletic training program is the minimum educational requirement. However, a master's degree is increasingly preferred — and in many cases required — reflecting the profession's transition to the professional master's degree as the entry-level standard. If you hold a bachelor's degree and BOC certification earned before the transition, you remain eligible, but candidates with graduate degrees may receive preference.


Current CPR/AED certification is also universally required.


### North Carolina Athletic Trainer Licensure


North Carolina requires athletic trainers to hold a license issued by the North Carolina Board of Athletic Trainer Examiners. You must obtain or transfer your license before beginning work at Fort Liberty. The state requires BOC certification as a prerequisite for licensure.


<Callout type="tip">
If you are relocating from another state, begin the North Carolina licensure application process as soon as you accept a position. Processing times vary, and some contractors may assist with expediting the application. Confirm this with your employer during the offer stage.
</Callout>


### Security Clearance Considerations at Fort Liberty


Fort Liberty's significant special operations presence means that some athletic trainer positions require a security clearance. Roles supporting USASOC elements or JSOC-affiliated units may require a Secret clearance, and select positions may require Top Secret or higher.


If you have never held a security clearance, be prepared for a background investigation that examines your financial history, criminal record, foreign contacts, and personal conduct. The investigation can take several weeks to several months, and the sponsoring contractor or government agency initiates the process — you do not apply for a clearance independently.


Positions supporting conventional forces through the H2F program generally do not require a security clearance, though a favorable background check is still standard.


### Defense Health Agency Credentialing and Privileging Process


Regardless of whether you hold a GS or contractor position, athletic trainers practicing in Department of Defense healthcare settings must complete the Defense Health Agency (DHA) credentialing and privileging process. This process verifies your education, certification, licensure, malpractice history, and clinical competence.


Credentialing through the DHA is separate from your BOC certification and state licensure. It authorizes you to practice within the military treatment facility's scope of care. Your employer will guide you through the application, but gathering the following documents in advance accelerates the timeline:


- BOC certification verification
- North Carolina license (or proof of application)
- Official transcripts
- Malpractice insurance documentation (if applicable)
- Professional references (typically three clinical references)
- Continuing education records


### Preferred Experience: Tactical and Operational Settings


While not always a hard requirement, hiring managers at Fort Liberty strongly prefer candidates with experience in tactical, operational, or high-performance athletic settings. Relevant experience includes:


- Previous military or Department of Defense athletic training roles
- Collegiate or professional sports medicine
- Tactical strength and conditioning (TSAC) or law enforcement human performance programs
- Orthopedic or sports medicine clinical rotations with high-volume musculoskeletal caseloads


Candidates holding additional credentials such as the CSCS (Certified Strength and Conditioning Specialist) or TSAC-F (Tactical Strength and Conditioning Facilitator) may have a competitive advantage, particularly for special operations support roles.


## Salary, Benefits, and Cost of Living in the Fayetteville Area


Compensation is a critical factor in your decision, and the financial picture at Fort Liberty is more favorable than the base salary numbers might initially suggest.


### Expected Salary Ranges for Contract and GS Athletic Trainers


Contract athletic trainer positions at Fort Liberty typically offer salaries in the range of $55,000 to $85,000 annually. The wide range reflects differences in contractor, specific unit assignment, years of experience, and whether the role supports conventional or special operations forces.


GS positions generally fall within the GS-9 to GS-11 pay grades, with base salaries ranging from approximately $49,000 to $73,000. The Fayetteville, NC, locality pay adjustment adds a percentage on top of the base salary, though the adjustment for this area is modest compared to major metropolitan regions.

Private contractor benefits packages for athletic trainers at military installations commonly include:

- Paid time off (vacation and sick leave)

- Life insurance
- Professional development allowances or continuing education support
- Relocation assistance (varies by contract and employer)


Specific benefits vary by contractor. PSI's recruitment team can provide detailed benefits information for current Fort Liberty openings during the application process.


### Cost of Living in Fayetteville and Surrounding Communities


Fayetteville, North Carolina, has a cost of living approximately 12 to 15 percent below the national average, according to cost-of-living indices. This differential significantly enhances the purchasing power of athletic trainer salaries at Fort Liberty compared to equivalent roles in higher-cost metropolitan areas.


Housing costs drive much of this advantage. The median home price in Fayetteville and surrounding communities like Spring Lake, Sanford, and Southern Pines remains well below the national median. Rental rates are similarly favorable.


### Housing, Commute, and Quality of Life Near Fort Liberty


Fort Liberty has multiple access gates, and commute times vary depending on where you live and which part of the installation you report to. Most athletic trainers live in Fayetteville proper, Southern Pines, or the smaller communities immediately adjacent to the installation, with commute times typically ranging from 15 to 40 minutes.


The Fayetteville area offers a moderate climate, reasonable traffic patterns outside of gate rush hours, and access to outdoor recreation. Raleigh and the Research Triangle are approximately 60 to 70 miles northeast, providing additional cultural and recreational options for day trips or weekends.


## What a Typical Day Looks Like for an Athletic Trainer at Fort Liberty


Understanding the daily rhythm of military athletic training helps you evaluate whether this practice setting aligns with your professional goals and personal preferences.


### Working with Tactical Athletes: Airborne, Infantry, and Special Operators


Your patients at Fort Liberty are not traditional athletes. They are service members whose physical performance directly impacts mission readiness and survival. The 82nd Airborne Division conducts regular parachute jumps, forced marches, and live-fire exercises. Special operations personnel maintain even more demanding training cycles.


The injury profiles you encounter reflect these demands: lower extremity overuse injuries from rucking and running, upper extremity trauma from parachute landing falls, lumbar spine pathology from sustained load carriage, and acute musculoskeletal injuries from combatives and obstacle course training. You assess, treat, and manage these conditions in field environments as well as clinical settings.


### Injury Prevention, Rehabilitation, and Return-to-Duty Protocols


A significant portion of your work focuses on injury prevention. This includes movement screening, corrective exercise programming, load management education, and integration of evidence-based prevention protocols into unit physical training plans.


When injuries occur, you manage rehabilitation with a return-to-duty framework that differs from traditional return-to-play models. The goal is restoring the service member to full occupational capacity, which may include the ability to carry heavy loads, operate in austere environments, and perform under physical and cognitive stress simultaneously.


### Collaboration with the H2F Multidisciplinary Team


Athletic trainers in H2F teams work alongside strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists. This interdisciplinary model requires strong communication, defined role boundaries, and collaborative treatment planning.


You may co-lead injury prevention programs with the strength coach, coordinate rehabilitation progressions with the physical therapist, and consult with the dietitian on recovery nutrition strategies. The team-based approach provides professional enrichment and a broader scope of influence on service member health outcomes than many athletic trainers experience in traditional settings.


### Unique Challenges and Rewards of Military Athletic Training


The military environment presents challenges that civilian sports medicine does not. Service members may resist reporting injuries due to career concerns. Operational timelines may compress rehabilitation windows. Field conditions may limit your equipment and facility access.


At the same time, the work carries a sense of purpose that many athletic trainers find deeply motivating. You contribute directly to the readiness of the nation's most capable military formations. The clinical complexity is high, the patient population is motivated, and the professional growth opportunities are substantial.


## How to Apply for Athletic Trainer Jobs at Fort Liberty


Knowing where and how to apply can mean the difference between a timely hire and months of waiting.


### Where to Find Current Openings (USAJobs, Contractor Websites, PSI)


Athletic trainer jobs at Fort Liberty are posted across several platforms:


- **USAJobs.gov:** The official federal hiring platform for GS positions. Search using keywords like "athletic trainer," "health technician," or "human performance" with Fort Liberty or Fort Bragg as the location.
- **Contractor websites:** Private military healthcare contractors post openings on their own career pages. PSI maintains current listings for athletic trainer positions at Fort Liberty and other installations on its website.
- **Industry job boards:** Sites such as the NATA Career Center, Indeed, and LinkedIn may aggregate both GS and contractor listings.


<Callout type="tip">
Set up email alerts on USAJobs and contractor career pages for Fort Liberty athletic trainer positions. Openings can be posted and filled quickly, especially for contract roles. Consistent monitoring gives you an advantage over candidates who search intermittently.
</Callout>


### Tips for Tailoring Your Resume for Military AT Positions


Military healthcare hiring managers and contractor recruiters look for specific signals in your resume:


- **Lead with credentials.** Place your BOC certification number, state licensure, and any additional certifications (CSCS, TSAC-F) prominently in your header or summary.
- **Quantify your experience.** Specify patient volumes, team sizes, and program outcomes wherever possible.
- **Use relevant terminology.** Reference injury prevention, return-to-duty, musculoskeletal assessment, and multidisciplinary collaboration. If you have experience with tactical populations, name it explicitly.
- **Address the requirements directly.** If the posting lists specific qualifications, mirror that language in your experience descriptions where truthful.
- **For USAJobs applications:** Federal resumes are significantly more detailed than private-sector resumes. Include hours worked per week, supervisor contact information, and full descriptions of duties for each position.


### What to Expect During the Interview and Hiring Process


Contract positions typically involve a phone or video screening with a recruiter, followed by one or two interviews with the program manager or site lead. Questions tend to focus on clinical competence, experience with tactical or athletic populations, comfort with autonomous practice, and adaptability to military culture.


GS positions follow the federal hiring process, which includes application scoring, referral to the hiring manager, and one or more structured interviews. The federal process is generally slower and more formalized.


### Timeline: From Application to Start Date


For contract positions through organizations like PSI, the timeline from application to start date can range from four to eight weeks, assuming your credentials are current and no security clearance is required. If a clearance is needed, add several weeks to several months.


GS positions on USAJobs typically take three to six months from application to start date, reflecting the federal hiring bureaucracy and background investigation process.
