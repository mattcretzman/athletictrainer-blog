# Athletic Trainer Jobs in Colorado: Fort Carson & Military Healthcare Careers

Colorado has become one of the most desirable states for athletic trainers seeking careers in military healthcare, anchored by Fort Carson — a major Army installation located on the southern edge of Colorado Springs. The combination of a growing military healthcare presence, access to world-class outdoor recreation, and a quality of life that consistently ranks among the best in the nation makes Colorado a compelling destination for certified athletic trainers.

Cognito Systems, a joint venture of Resolution Think and QuarterLine, staffed by PSI, connects athletic trainers with positions at Fort Carson and other military installations. These roles are primarily available through the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program, offering team-based practice, professional autonomy, and direct impact on service member readiness.

## Why Colorado for Military Athletic Training

### Fort Carson: The State's Primary Military AT Employer

Fort Carson is Colorado's largest military installation and the primary source of athletic trainer positions in the state. Located at the base of the Rocky Mountains in Colorado Springs, Fort Carson is home to the 4th Infantry Division and multiple supporting units. The installation's high operational tempo and the Army's investment in the H2F program have created a steady and growing demand for certified athletic trainers.

Colorado is also home to the U.S. Air Force Academy in Colorado Springs, Peterson Space Force Base, Schriever Space Force Base, Buckley Space Force Base near Denver, and NORAD at Cheyenne Mountain. While most current athletic trainer positions are concentrated at Fort Carson, the broader military presence in Colorado represents potential for future expansion as performance optimization programs grow across branches.

### The Colorado Lifestyle

Colorado consistently ranks among the top states in the country for quality of life, outdoor recreation, health and wellness, and overall livability. The state's Front Range corridor — stretching from Fort Collins through Denver to Colorado Springs — combines mountain access, urban amenities, and a culture that values physical fitness and outdoor activity. For athletic trainers, living in a state where the general population prioritizes health and performance creates a natural alignment between professional life and personal values.

### Altitude and Environmental Considerations

Colorado Springs sits at approximately 6,035 feet of elevation, and Fort Carson's training areas extend into higher terrain. Working at altitude introduces unique considerations for athletic trainers — including altitude acclimatization for service members arriving from lower elevations, modified hydration and exertion protocols, and awareness of altitude-related illness in field training environments. Athletic trainers at Fort Carson develop specialized expertise in altitude-related performance optimization that distinguishes their professional experience.

## Fort Carson: The Heart of Colorado Military Athletic Training

### Installation Overview

Fort Carson occupies approximately 137,000 acres along the southern boundary of Colorado Springs. The installation is home to the 4th Infantry Division ("Ivy Division"), the 10th Special Forces Group, the 71st Ordnance Group, and numerous other units. With approximately 25,000 to 28,000 active-duty service members, Fort Carson generates consistent demand for healthcare professionals, including athletic trainers.

The 4th Infantry Division maintains a high operational tempo, with units frequently engaged in deployment preparation cycles, field training exercises, and combat training center rotations. This translates into a steady caseload of musculoskeletal injuries, movement dysfunctions, and performance optimization needs — the core of athletic training practice.

### H2F Program at Fort Carson

The H2F program at Fort Carson embeds interdisciplinary performance teams directly into Brigade Combat Teams. Each team includes athletic trainers, strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, registered dietitians, occupational therapists, and cognitive performance specialists. Athletic trainers are assigned to specific brigades and work directly with service members in their training environments.

Fort Carson has been one of the priority installations for H2F expansion, and the program continues to grow as new brigades are brought into the system. Athletic trainers here work alongside a diverse team of performance professionals, creating an environment of interdisciplinary collaboration that fosters professional growth and reduces the professional isolation common in many traditional athletic training settings.

### Daily Work at Fort Carson

Athletic trainers at Fort Carson follow a consistent Monday through Friday schedule, typically from daytime hours, with 40-hour work weeks. There are no on-call requirements, no weekend shifts, and no travel obligations. This schedule represents a fundamental departure from the demands of collegiate or professional sports athletic training, where 60-hour weeks and constant travel are standard.

A typical day begins with morning physical training alongside the assigned unit. Athletic trainers are present on the PT field to observe movement patterns, provide immediate assessment of injuries that occur during training, and conduct injury prevention screenings. The remainder of the day includes scheduled evaluations, treatment sessions, rehabilitation progressions, and coordination with the performance team and military medical providers.

The work is autonomous. Athletic trainers conduct their own evaluations, develop treatment plans, manage return-to-duty progressions, and make clinical decisions based on their professional judgment. The military healthcare model prioritizes readiness outcomes over billing metrics, allowing athletic trainers to focus on patient care rather than insurance documentation.

### Evans Army Community Hospital

Fort Carson's Evans Army Community Hospital provides additional athletic training opportunities in the outpatient orthopedic and sports medicine clinics. These clinic-based roles follow a more traditional healthcare model with scheduled appointments and close collaboration with orthopedic surgeons, physical therapists, and other medical providers.

## Living in Colorado Springs

### City Overview

Colorado Springs is a city of approximately 490,000 residents (nearly 750,000 in the metro area), making it the second-largest city in Colorado after Denver. The city sits at the eastern base of Pikes Peak and the Front Range of the Rocky Mountains, creating a dramatic natural setting that defines daily life. Colorado Springs has a strong military community culture — driven by Fort Carson, the Air Force Academy, Peterson Space Force Base, and other installations — making it a welcoming environment for those working in military healthcare.

### Outdoor Recreation

The outdoor recreation opportunities in Colorado Springs are exceptional. Garden of the Gods, a world-famous public park with dramatic red rock formations, is minutes from downtown. Pikes Peak (14,115 feet) is accessible by car, foot, or cog railway. The city maintains over 100 miles of urban trail, and access to hiking, mountain biking, rock climbing, skiing, snowboarding, fishing, and camping is available year-round.

For athletic trainers who value an active lifestyle, Colorado Springs is difficult to beat. The early-afternoon finish time of military healthcare positions means that there are several hours of daylight remaining for outdoor pursuits, even during the shorter days of winter.

### Climate and Weather

Colorado Springs has a semi-arid climate with approximately 300 days of sunshine per year. Summers are warm but not excessively hot, with average highs in the low 80s and cool evenings. Winters bring cold temperatures and occasional snow, but the city's low humidity and abundant sunshine mean that snow often melts quickly. The climate supports year-round outdoor activity, with skiing accessible in the mountains to the west during winter months.

### Community and Culture

Colorado Springs has a growing food scene, a thriving arts district in the downtown core, and a range of dining, entertainment, and cultural options. The city's Olympic and Paralympic Training Center (now the U.S. Olympic and Paralympic Museum) reflects the community's emphasis on athletic performance and human optimization — values that align naturally with the athletic training profession.

Denver is approximately 70 miles to the north, accessible via Interstate 25, providing additional entertainment, professional sports, airport access, and cultural options for weekend trips.

### Housing and Cost of Living

Colorado Springs' cost of living sits moderately above the national average, primarily driven by housing costs that have increased significantly over the past decade. However, it remains considerably more affordable than Denver, and competitive compensation for military healthcare positions helps offset the premium. For athletic trainers relocating from higher-cost markets on the East or West Coast, Colorado Springs may actually represent a cost-of-living improvement.

## Colorado Athletic Trainer Licensure

Colorado does not currently require a state-specific athletic trainer license. Athletic trainers must maintain current BOC certification to practice. This streamlined regulatory environment can be an advantage for athletic trainers relocating from other states, as there is no additional state licensure application or fee required. As always, candidates should verify current requirements during the application process, as regulatory landscapes can change.

## How to Apply for Athletic Trainer Jobs in Colorado

### Requirements

- Current BOC certification
- Master's degree from a CAATE-accredited program (for positions requiring it)
- Eligibility for a Department of Defense security clearance
- U.S. citizenship (required for most government contract positions)

### Application Process

Cognito Systems coordinates placement for athletic trainers at Fort Carson and other military installations. The process includes credential verification, security clearance initiation, and matching candidates to available positions. Fort Carson positions tend to be popular due to the location's desirability, so expressing interest early and maintaining current credentials can position candidates well when openings arise.

### Security Clearance Timeline

Security clearance processing varies but typically takes several weeks to several months. Starting the clearance process early — even before a specific position is confirmed — can help reduce wait times and accelerate placement.
