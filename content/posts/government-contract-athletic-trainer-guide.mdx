## What Government Contract AT Positions Actually Are

When most athletic trainers hear "government job," they picture a GS-scale federal employee sitting in a VA hospital. Government contract athletic trainer positions are something different entirely.

Contract ATs are employed by private companies that hold contracts with the Department of Defense to provide healthcare services at military installations. You work on a military base, you treat active-duty service members, and your work is funded by the federal government. But your paycheck comes from the contracting company, not the government itself.

This distinction matters because it affects everything from how you are hired to how you are paid to how quickly you can start working. Government contract positions move faster, pay competitively, and offer more flexibility than direct federal employment.

Cognito Systems, a joint venture of Resolution Think and QuarterLine, staffed by Planned Systems International (PSI), is one of the primary contractors managing athletic trainer positions within the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program and the Marine Corps' Sports Medicine Injury Prevention (SMIP) program.

## How the Contracting Model Works

### The Contract Structure

The Department of Defense awards multi-year contracts to companies that meet specific capability, experience, and performance requirements. These contracts specify the number of athletic trainers needed, the installations where they will serve, the qualifications required, and the standards of care expected.

The contracting company is responsible for recruiting, hiring, credentialing, and managing the athletic trainers who fill these positions. The company handles payroll, benefits administration, professional development support, and day-to-day employment matters. The military installation provides the clinical space, the patient population, and the operational framework within which athletic trainers practice.

### What This Means for You

As a contract athletic trainer, your employment relationship is with the contracting company. You apply through the company, you are interviewed by the company's talent acquisition team, and your offer letter comes from the company. The military installation's chain of command provides clinical direction and operational guidance, but your employer is the contractor.

This model provides several advantages. Hiring decisions are made by the contracting company based on clinical qualifications and fit, not by a federal HR bureaucracy with rigid classification requirements. Compensation packages are set by the contractor and are generally competitive with or above market rates. And the onboarding timeline is measured in weeks, not the months-long process that characterizes direct federal hiring.

## Security and Background Requirements

### What Is Required

All athletic trainers working on military installations must pass a background investigation. The level of investigation depends on the specific position and installation, but most H2F and SMIP positions require a favorable adjudication of a National Agency Check with Inquiries (NACI) or equivalent background check.

This is not a security clearance in the classified information sense. You are not handling state secrets. The background investigation verifies your identity, checks criminal history, confirms employment and education history, and evaluates whether you present any disqualifying factors for access to a military installation.

### What Is Not Required

You do not need an existing security clearance to apply. The background investigation is initiated after you accept a position, and the contracting company manages the process. You do not need to be a veteran. You do not need prior government employment. You do not need to have worked on a military base before.

The most common concern candidates express about the background check is whether minor issues in their past will disqualify them. In general, the investigation looks for patterns of serious criminal behavior, financial irresponsibility, or dishonesty, not minor traffic violations or a college-era misdemeanor. If you have specific concerns, the talent acquisition team can provide guidance during the application process.

## Benefits and Compensation

### Salary

Government contract athletic trainer salaries are competitive with clinical and collegiate AT positions and often exceed them when total compensation is considered. Exact figures vary by installation location, cost of living, and candidate experience, but the compensation is designed to attract and retain qualified practitioners in a competitive market.

### Benefits Package

Contract AT positions include comprehensive benefits that many athletic trainers in private practice settings do not receive:

- Health, dental, and vision insurance
- Retirement plan with employer contributions
- Paid time off and federal holidays
- Life insurance and disability coverage
- Relocation assistance for many positions

### Credential Support

One of the most distinctive benefits of government contract AT positions is full credential support. Your employer covers:

- BOC certification renewal fees
- NATA membership dues
- State licensure costs and renewals
- Continuing education allowance (typically $500 annually for CEU courses and conferences)

For athletic trainers who have been paying these costs out of pocket throughout their careers, the credential support package alone represents a meaningful improvement in effective compensation.

## The Hiring Process

### Step 1: Initial Contact

The process begins when you submit your information through the contracting company's recruitment portal. A member of the talent acquisition team will reach out to discuss your background, preferences, and available positions. This initial conversation is informational, not evaluative. The goal is to understand what you are looking for and identify positions that match.

### Step 2: Credential Verification

The company verifies your BOC certification, state licensure, education, and employment history. This step is straightforward if your credentials are current and in good standing.

### Step 3: Position Matching

Based on your preferences, qualifications, and available openings, the talent acquisition team presents specific position options. You will know the installation, the unit, the team structure, and the start timeline before making a decision.

### Step 4: Background Investigation

Once you accept a position, the background investigation process begins. The contracting company initiates the paperwork and guides you through each step. The timeline varies but typically takes two to six weeks.

### Step 5: Onboarding and Start

After favorable adjudication of your background check, you complete onboarding with the contracting company and report to your assigned installation. Many positions include relocation support if you are moving from out of the area.

## Common Questions

### Do I have to move?

That depends on where you live and which positions are available. With more than 30 installations across the country, there may be a position within commuting distance of your current location. If not, relocation assistance is available for many positions.

### Can I choose my location?

Yes. You are not assigned to a random installation. The talent acquisition team works with you to identify positions at locations that fit your preferences and life circumstances.

### What if I want to change locations later?

One of the advantages of the contracting model is geographic mobility. Athletic trainers can request transfers to other installations as positions become available. Your career is not anchored to a single location.

### Is this a temporary job?

No. Government contracts are typically multi-year agreements, and the H2F program is expanding, not contracting. These are career positions with long-term stability, not short-term gigs.
