## Army H2F Jobs Are Civilian Positions — Here Is How to Get One

The most common misconception about Army H2F jobs is that they require enlisting. They do not. Athletic trainers working in the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness program are civilian employees. They do not wear a uniform, they do not go through basic training, and they do not deploy. They go to work on an Army installation, do their job as credentialed healthcare professionals, and go home at the end of the day.

This guide walks through the entire process of getting hired for an Army H2F athletic trainer position — from understanding what the role entails, to finding open positions, to navigating the application and interview process, to knowing what to expect once an offer is extended.

## Understanding the H2F Program

Before applying, it helps to understand what H2F actually is and how athletic trainers fit into the program.

The Holistic Health and Fitness program is the Army's primary system for improving soldier readiness and reducing injury rates across the force. It operates at the brigade level, embedding multidisciplinary human performance teams directly into Army units. Each H2F team typically includes athletic trainers, strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists.

The athletic trainer's role within H2F is focused on injury prevention, musculoskeletal evaluation, rehabilitation, movement screening, and return-to-duty decision-making. It is clinical work performed within a team-based model — the same scope of practice an AT would exercise in any high-level sports medicine setting, applied to the unique demands of military service members.

H2F is not a pilot program or a temporary initiative. It has been funded, expanded, and directed by Congress to reach every brigade combat team in the active-duty Army. That expansion is ongoing and is creating a growing number of athletic trainer positions at installations across the United States and overseas.

## Who Is Eligible

Army H2F athletic trainer positions have straightforward eligibility requirements. Candidates must hold:

- A **master's degree** in athletic training or a closely related field (this aligns with the profession's current entry-level degree requirement)
- **BOC certification** (Board of Certification for the Athletic Trainer)
- **State licensure** appropriate to the state where the installation is located (or eligibility to obtain it)
- **U.S. citizenship** or authorization to work in the United States

Prior military experience is not required. Prior experience working with military populations is not required. The positions are open to athletic trainers from any background — secondary school, collegiate, clinical, professional sports, or other settings.

What matters is clinical competence, the ability to work within a team, and a genuine interest in the mission.

## Where to Find Army H2F Positions

Army H2F athletic trainer positions are filled through Cognito Systems, a joint venture of Resolution Think and QuarterLine, staffed by PSI. Cognito Systems holds the contract for H2F staffing and is the hiring entity for these roles.

The most direct way to find and apply for current openings is through [athletictrainerjob.com](https://athletictrainerjob.com). This site lists available positions by location, provides details about the role, and offers a streamlined application process.

### Why You Will Not Find These on USAJobs

A common point of confusion: Army H2F positions are not federal civil service jobs posted on USAJobs.gov. They are contract positions filled through Cognito Systems. The application process is faster, more straightforward, and does not involve the lengthy federal hiring timeline that USAJobs applicants are accustomed to.

This is an important distinction. Athletic trainers who have searched for "H2F" or "athletic trainer" on USAJobs and found nothing may have assumed the positions do not exist or are not currently hiring. That is not the case. The positions exist. They are actively hiring. They are just filled through a different channel.

## The Application Process: Step by Step

### Step 1: Review Available Positions

Start at [athletictrainerjob.com](https://athletictrainerjob.com) and review the current openings. Positions are listed by installation, and each listing includes details about the location, the role, and the requirements.

Take note of which locations are available. Army installations with H2F teams span the continental United States and extend to overseas locations including Hawaii, Japan, Germany, Italy, and South Korea. The range of locations is one of the unique aspects of H2F employment — an athletic trainer can build a career at a single installation or pursue opportunities at different posts over time.

### Step 2: Submit Your Application

The application itself is straightforward. Candidates submit their resume, credentials (BOC certification, state licensure or eligibility), and basic professional information through the application portal.

A few practical tips for the application:

- **Be specific about your clinical experience.** The hiring team wants to understand your hands-on clinical background — injury evaluation, rehabilitation program design, emergency care, and population health management. Highlight these areas clearly.

- **Emphasize team-based experience.** H2F is built around multidisciplinary collaboration. If you have worked alongside physical therapists, strength coaches, physicians, or other allied health professionals, make that clear.

- **Do not undersell non-traditional experience.** Athletic trainers coming from secondary schools, industrial settings, or performing arts have clinical skills that transfer directly to the military healthcare environment. The population is different, but the clinical reasoning is the same.

### Step 3: Initial Screening

After submission, the Cognito Systems recruitment team reviews applications and conducts an initial screening. This is a relatively quick process compared to traditional healthcare or government hiring timelines. Qualified candidates are typically contacted within a matter of weeks, not months.

### Step 4: The Interview

The interview process for Army H2F positions is thorough but not adversarial. It is designed to assess clinical competence, cultural fit, and genuine interest in the mission.

Greg LaDue, who leads the hiring process for Cognito Systems, has noted that the interview serves a dual purpose. It is an opportunity for the hiring team to evaluate the candidate, but it is also an opportunity for the candidate to learn about the role. The interview includes education about what H2F is, what the daily work looks like, and what the employment experience involves.

This is worth emphasizing because it reflects the hiring philosophy. Cognito Systems is not just trying to fill seats. The goal is to match the right athletic trainers with the right positions in a way that sets both the AT and the program up for long-term success. Candidates should come prepared with questions — about the clinical environment, the team structure, the installation, and the day-to-day expectations.

**Common interview topics include:**

- Clinical scenarios related to musculoskeletal injury evaluation and management
- Approach to working within a multidisciplinary team
- Experience with injury prevention programming at the population level
- Interest in the military healthcare mission and working with service members
- Questions about schedule expectations, location preferences, and career goals

### Step 5: Location Selection

One of the most distinctive aspects of the Army H2F hiring process is the ability to express location preferences. Unlike many healthcare positions where the employer dictates the location, H2F candidates have input into where they are assigned.

That said, it is important to set realistic expectations about location availability. Some installations are in higher demand than others. Locations in Hawaii, Japan, and California tend to have waitlists because of their desirability. Installations in less sought-after locations may have immediate availability.

Athletic trainers who are flexible on location will generally move through the process faster and have more options. Those with a strong preference for a specific installation should communicate that clearly and understand that timing may vary.

### Step 6: Offer and Onboarding

Once a candidate is selected, Cognito Systems extends an offer that includes details on compensation, benefits, start date, and installation assignment. The onboarding process covers everything an athletic trainer needs to know about working on a military installation as a civilian employee — access procedures, facility orientation, team introductions, and program-specific training.

The transition from offer to start date is designed to be smooth. Athletic trainers who are relocating for the position receive support in understanding the installation and surrounding community.

## What to Expect as a Civilian Employee

### You Are Not Enlisting

This point cannot be made often enough. Army H2F athletic trainers are civilian employees. The relationship to the military is professional, not contractual in the enlistment sense. ATs work on military installations alongside service members and military staff, but they maintain their civilian status throughout.

There is no boot camp. There is no rank. There is no deployment. There is clinical work, performed by a credentialed healthcare professional, within a structured and supportive environment.

### The Schedule

Athletic trainers in H2F work 40-hour weeks, Monday through Friday. Standard hours are daytime hours There are no evening obligations, no weekend coverage requirements, and no on-call expectations.

For athletic trainers coming from traditional settings where 50- to 60-hour weeks are the norm, this schedule represents one of the most significant quality-of-life improvements available in the profession. The early start time means the workday ends early, leaving the afternoon and evening free.

### The Team

H2F teams are genuinely multidisciplinary. Athletic trainers work daily alongside other ATs, strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists. The team structure means that clinical decisions are collaborative, coverage is shared, and no single AT bears the burden of sole responsibility for an entire population.

This team-based approach is one of the most frequently cited positive aspects of H2F employment. Athletic trainers who have spent years as the only AT in a building describe the experience of working with peers as transformative.

### The Clinical Environment

The clinical work in H2F is substantive and challenging. Service members are physically active, high-demand individuals with injury profiles that require advanced clinical reasoning. The musculoskeletal evaluation, rehabilitation, and injury prevention work is at a level that keeps experienced athletic trainers professionally engaged.

ATs in H2F work with advanced equipment and have access to facilities that are purpose-built for human performance. The clinical environment supports the kind of thorough, evidence-based practice that many athletic trainers aspire to but cannot achieve in understaffed traditional settings.

### Benefits

Army H2F positions through Cognito Systems come with competitive compensation and a comprehensive benefits package. While specific details are provided during the offer stage, candidates can expect the kind of benefits structure associated with a major Department of Defense contractor — health insurance, retirement contributions, paid time off, and continuing education support.

## Common Questions from Candidates

### Do I need military experience?

No. Prior military experience is not required and is not expected. The hiring team evaluates candidates based on clinical competence, professional credentials, and fit for the role. Athletic trainers from every background — schools, clinics, colleges, professional sports, industrial settings — have successfully transitioned into H2F.

### Can I choose my location?

Candidates can express location preferences, and the hiring process takes those preferences into account. However, availability varies by installation. Some locations are immediately available while others have waitlists. Flexibility on location increases the speed and range of options.

### Is this a government job?

H2F athletic trainer positions are contract positions through Cognito Systems, not federal civil service positions. The hiring process is separate from USAJobs and follows a different timeline and structure.

### What if I have a family?

Many athletic trainers in H2F have families, and the 40-hour, Monday-through-Friday schedule is one of the reasons they chose military healthcare. The predictable hours and the absence of evening and weekend obligations make H2F one of the most family-compatible AT roles available.

For those assigned to installations with on-post housing and family support services, additional resources are available through the installation community.

### How long does the hiring process take?

Timelines vary based on position availability, location preferences, and background screening requirements. In general, the process from application to offer is significantly faster than traditional government hiring. Candidates should expect to hear back after initial screening within a few weeks, with the full process from application to start date taking a matter of weeks to a few months depending on circumstances.

### What about career growth?

H2F is an expanding program. As it scales to reach every brigade combat team in the Army, the need for experienced athletic trainers in supervisory and program leadership roles grows in parallel. Athletic trainers who enter H2F at the clinical level have opportunities to advance into site lead, regional coordination, and program management positions over time.

## The Path Forward

Army H2F jobs represent one of the most significant opportunities in the athletic training profession today. The positions are real, they are growing in number, and they offer a combination of clinical challenge, team-based support, sustainable hours, and competitive compensation that is difficult to find in traditional settings.

The application process is straightforward. The eligibility requirements are clear. The hiring team is actively looking for qualified athletic trainers who are ready to bring their skills to a new environment.

The only step left is the first one.
