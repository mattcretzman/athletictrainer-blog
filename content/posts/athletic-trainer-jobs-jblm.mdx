# Athletic Trainer Jobs at Joint Base Lewis-McChord: Living and Working in the Pacific Northwest

If you are a certified athletic trainer considering a career in military healthcare, Joint Base Lewis-McChord (JBLM) represents one of the most compelling opportunities in the country. Located in Washington state's South Puget Sound region, JBLM combines a high-demand clinical environment with a quality of life that few other military installations can match.

This guide covers everything you need to evaluate athletic trainer jobs at Joint Base Lewis-McChord — from the specific roles and units you would support to the salary, credentialing requirements, and what daily life looks like in the Pacific Northwest.

## Why Joint Base Lewis-McChord Is a Top Destination for Athletic Trainers

Athletic trainers at Joint Base Lewis-McChord work as embedded healthcare professionals within Army brigade combat teams under the Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program. They provide injury prevention, musculoskeletal assessments, rehabilitation, and performance optimization services to active-duty service members across units including I Corps, 7th Infantry Division, and Special Operations forces.

### JBLM Overview: The Largest Military Installation on the West Coast

Joint Base Lewis-McChord is the largest military installation on the West Coast, housing over 40,000 active-duty service members and supporting approximately 120,000 military-connected residents. The installation encompasses both the Army's Fort Lewis and the Air Force's McChord Field, creating a joint operations environment with a massive healthcare footprint.

Madigan Army Medical Center, one of the Department of Defense's largest medical facilities, is located on base and serves as a regional healthcare hub. This means athletic trainers at JBLM operate within a well-resourced medical ecosystem, with access to advanced imaging, orthopedic specialists, and interdisciplinary collaboration that smaller installations simply cannot offer.

### Key Military Units and the Demand for Athletic Trainers

The concentration of high-readiness units at JBLM drives significant and sustained demand for athletic trainers. The installation is home to:

- **I Corps** — the Army's premier deployable corps headquarters in the Pacific
- **7th Infantry Division** — multiple brigade combat teams with thousands of soldiers
- **1st Special Forces Group (Airborne)** — requiring elite-level performance programming
- **2nd Battalion, 75th Ranger Regiment** — one of the most physically demanding units in the military
- **Joint personnel from McChord Field** — including air mobility and special operations aviation units

Each of these units places distinct physical demands on service members, from sustained endurance requirements in infantry roles to explosive power demands in special operations. This diversity means your clinical caseload at JBLM would be both challenging and varied — far more so than at installations with a single dominant unit type.

### How the H2F Program Has Expanded Athletic Trainer Opportunities at JBLM

The Army's [Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program](/blog/h2f-program-athletic-trainer-positions), which began full-scale implementation in 2020 and continues expanding across all brigade combat teams, has been the single largest driver of athletic trainer hiring at military installations. JBLM, with its multiple brigade-sized elements, has been a primary beneficiary of this expansion.

Under H2F, each brigade combat team receives an embedded performance team that includes certified athletic trainers, strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, dietitians, and cognitive performance specialists. For athletic trainers, this translates into defined positions with clear scopes of practice and genuine integration into the military unit's operational rhythm.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics projects athletic trainer employment to grow 14% from 2022 to 2032 — much faster than average — and military tactical performance roles represent a significant portion of that growth. JBLM's size means it accounts for a meaningful share of H2F athletic trainer positions nationwide.

## Athletic Trainer Job Roles and Responsibilities at JBLM

Understanding the day-to-day work is essential before committing to a relocation. Athletic trainer roles at JBLM differ meaningfully from traditional sports medicine positions, though the foundational clinical skills transfer directly.

### Embedded Athletic Trainer Roles Within Brigade Combat Teams

As an embedded athletic trainer within an H2F team at JBLM, you would be assigned to a specific brigade combat team rather than working in a centralized clinic. This means your workspace is at the unit's physical training areas, battalion aid stations, and field environments — not a traditional athletic training room.

Your primary responsibilities typically include:

- **Musculoskeletal injury evaluation and triage** at the point of occurrence
- **Acute care and emergency management** during physical training events and field exercises
- **Therapeutic rehabilitation** for service members recovering from orthopedic injuries
- **Movement screening and functional assessments** to identify injury risk factors
- **Educational programming** on injury prevention, load management, and recovery strategies
- **Documentation and tracking** of injury data to inform unit-level decisions

### Injury Prevention and Musculoskeletal Screening Programs

Musculoskeletal injuries remain the leading cause of limited duty days across the Army. At JBLM, athletic trainers play a central role in screening programs designed to reduce these injuries before they occur. This work involves conducting standardized movement assessments on incoming soldiers, building individualized corrective exercise programs, and advising military leadership on training modifications that reduce injury rates without compromising readiness.

For athletic trainers who find reactive care unfulfilling, this prevention-oriented model can be deeply rewarding. You are not simply treating injuries after they happen — you are actively reshaping how a unit approaches physical performance.

### Collaboration with Physical Therapists, Strength Coaches, and Military Leadership

The H2F model is fundamentally interdisciplinary. At JBLM, you would work alongside physical therapists from Madigan Army Medical Center, strength and conditioning coaches certified through the NSCA or CSCCa, registered dietitians, and occupational therapy professionals. Daily collaboration is the norm, not the exception.

Equally important is your relationship with military leadership. Company commanders and first sergeants rely on the embedded athletic trainer for actionable intelligence about their unit's physical status. Your ability to communicate clinical findings in operational terms — not medical jargon — directly influences how seriously your recommendations are taken.

### How Military Athletic Trainer Roles Differ from Traditional Sports Medicine Positions

If you are transitioning from a collegiate or clinical setting, several differences are worth noting:

- **Population scope:** You serve an entire brigade (3,000-5,000 service members), not a single team roster
- **Autonomy:** Embedded positions afford greater clinical autonomy than many traditional settings
- **Environment:** Much of your work happens outdoors, in tactical scenarios, or in austere field conditions
- **Culture:** You are a civilian embedded within a military command structure, which requires adaptability and cultural competence
- **Impact measurement:** Success is measured in duty days preserved and unit readiness metrics, not win-loss records

## Qualifications and Requirements for JBLM Athletic Trainer Positions

Athletic trainer jobs at Joint Base Lewis-McChord carry specific credentialing, education, and clearance requirements. Meeting these before you apply streamlines the hiring process considerably.

### BOC Certification and Education Requirements

All athletic trainer positions at JBLM require current certification from the Board of Certification (BOC). You must have completed a CAATE-accredited athletic training program, and most positions require a minimum of a master's degree, reflecting the [current professional standards](/blog/athletic-trainer-certification-requirements-military-jobs) in the field.

Maintaining your BOC certification, including continuing education requirements, is your responsibility throughout the contract period.

### Washington State Athletic Trainer Licensure

Washington state requires athletic trainers to hold a license issued by the Washington State Department of Health. The application process involves verifying your BOC certification, submitting educational transcripts, and passing a background check at the state level.

A nuance worth understanding: federal contractor positions on military installations may operate under federal jurisdiction, which can affect how state licensure requirements are applied. However, most contractors hiring for JBLM positions require Washington state licensure or proof that you have initiated the application process. Securing your Washington license before applying strengthens your candidacy and avoids delays during onboarding.

<Callout type="tip">
Begin the Washington state licensure application as soon as you are seriously considering JBLM positions. The Department of Health processing timeline can vary, and having your license in hand — or at least your application on file — demonstrates readiness and can accelerate your start date.
</Callout>

### Security Clearance and Background Check Requirements

Working on a military installation requires at minimum a favorable background investigation. Most H2F contract athletic trainer positions at JBLM require a National Agency Check with Inquiries (NACI) or equivalent, which examines criminal history, financial records, and employment history. Some positions supporting special operations units — such as 1st Special Forces Group or the Ranger Battalion — may require a higher-level security clearance.

The background investigation process is initiated by the hiring contractor after a conditional offer of employment. It can take several weeks to several months, so factor this into your timeline.

### Preferred Experience: Tactical and Military Population Knowledge

While not always required, experience with tactical or military populations gives you a meaningful advantage. Contractors and hiring managers look favorably on:

- Previous work at military installations or with Department of Defense programs
- Experience with tactical athlete populations (military, law enforcement, fire service)
- Familiarity with H2F program structure and documentation systems
- Proficiency in movement screening protocols (FMS, SFMA, Y-Balance)
- Experience in outdoor or austere clinical environments

If you lack direct military experience, that is not a disqualifier. Many athletic trainers successfully transition from collegiate or clinical settings into military performance roles. Your willingness to learn the culture and adapt your clinical approach matters more than a specific background.

## Salary, Benefits, and Government Contractor Employment at JBLM

Compensation is a practical consideration that deserves clear, honest treatment. Military contract athletic trainer positions at JBLM offer competitive pay, especially when you factor in Washington state's tax advantages.

### Typical Salary Ranges for Contract Athletic Trainers at JBLM

Based on current market data for the Tacoma-Lakewood metropolitan area, athletic trainer positions at JBLM typically fall within competitive ranges.

Compensation varies by installation location and contract terms. Competitive salary packages are offered across all experience levels.

Government contract positions at military installations are generally competitive with or above broader market rates for athletic trainers in the region. Contract positions at installations with special operations units may command a premium.

### Benefits Packages from Military Healthcare Contractors

Government contract positions typically include benefits such as:

- Health, dental, and vision insurance
- Paid time off and federal holidays
- 401(k) or equivalent retirement savings plans
- Continuing education allowances
- Credentialing support and professional development opportunities

Specific benefits vary by contractor, so reviewing the complete benefits package during the offer stage is important. PSI and other established military healthcare contractors generally offer comprehensive benefits comparable to direct healthcare employment.

### Washington State's No Income Tax Advantage

One of the most significant financial benefits of working in Washington state is the absence of a state income tax. For athletic trainers, this can represent significant additional take-home pay compared to states with moderate-to-high income tax rates like California, Oregon, or New York.

This tax advantage effectively closes the gap between JBLM contract salaries and higher nominal salaries in states with substantial tax burdens. When evaluating offers, compare take-home pay rather than gross salary.

### Understanding Government Contractor vs. GS Employee Positions

Most athletic trainer positions at JBLM are through government contractors rather than as direct federal employees (GS positions). Key differences include:

- **Contract positions** are employed by a private company (such as PSI) that holds a contract with the Department of Defense. Your employer is the contractor, not the government.
- **GS positions** are direct federal employee roles with different pay scales, benefits structures (FEHB, FERS), and hiring processes (USAJobs).
- Contract positions typically offer faster hiring timelines and more flexibility, while GS positions provide greater long-term job stability and federal retirement benefits.

Understanding which employment model fits your career goals helps you target the right opportunities.

## Living in the Pacific Northwest: Quality of Life Near JBLM

A job is only as good as the life it supports. The Pacific Northwest offers athletic trainers a lifestyle that aligns remarkably well with the profession's emphasis on physical activity, health, and outdoor engagement.

### Best Communities to Live Near JBLM: Tacoma, Lakewood, Olympia, and Beyond

JBLM sits between Tacoma and Olympia along the Interstate 5 corridor, giving you several distinct communities to consider:

- **Lakewood:** Directly adjacent to JBLM's main gate. Shortest commute, most affordable housing, and a growing restaurant and retail scene.
- **Tacoma:** Approximately 15-20 minutes north. A mid-sized city with a revitalized downtown, craft breweries, museums, and diverse neighborhoods. Offers urban amenities without Seattle pricing.
- **Olympia:** Washington's state capital, approximately 25 minutes south. College-town atmosphere with a strong outdoor culture and farmers market scene.
- **Lacey and Yelm:** Smaller communities south and southeast of JBLM offering more rural living and lower housing costs.
- **University Place and DuPont:** Quiet residential communities near base with good schools and family-friendly amenities.

### Cost of Living Breakdown for the South Puget Sound Region

The cost of living in the South Puget Sound region runs approximately 10-20% above the national average, driven primarily by housing costs. However, it remains significantly more affordable than the Seattle metro area, which sits 40-50% above the national average.

| Groceries | 5-10% higher |

An athletic trainer earning a competitive salary with no state income tax can live comfortably in most communities near JBLM. Dual-income households will find the region quite manageable.

### Outdoor Recreation: Mountains, Water, and Trails at Your Doorstep

This is where the Pacific Northwest truly distinguishes itself. Within 90 minutes of JBLM, you have access to:

- **Mount Rainier National Park** — world-class hiking, climbing, and backcountry skiing approximately 60 miles southeast
- **Olympic National Park** — temperate rainforests, alpine meadows, and rugged coastline across the Puget Sound
- **Puget Sound and the San Juan Islands** — kayaking, sailing, and whale watching
- **Crystal Mountain and White Pass** — ski resorts within a two-hour drive
- **Hundreds of miles of trail networks** — including the Foothills Trail, Nisqually River Trail, and Pacific Crest Trail access points

For athletic trainers who value physical activity outside of work, few locations offer this density of outdoor recreation. The landscape reinforces the active lifestyle that likely drew you to the profession in the first place.

### Climate, Culture, and What to Expect from PNW Living

The South Puget Sound climate is mild year-round, with average winter lows around 35F and summer highs around 75F. The region does receive substantial rainfall from October through April — approximately 40 inches annually — though summers are famously dry and temperate.

Culturally, the Pacific Northwest tends toward an outdoor-oriented, environmentally conscious, and independently minded identity. The food scene emphasizes local and seasonal ingredients. Coffee culture is, predictably, deeply embedded. Traffic on I-5 near Joint Base Lewis-McChord and Tacoma can be significant during peak commuting hours, so choosing a residence with commute times in mind is advisable.

## How to Land an Athletic Trainer Job at JBLM

Knowing the opportunity exists is one thing. Successfully navigating the application and hiring process is another. Here is how to position yourself effectively.

### Working with Military Healthcare Recruiters Like PSI

Government contracts for athletic trainer positions at JBLM are held by specialized military healthcare contractors. Working with a dedicated military healthcare recruiter streamlines the process because these recruiters understand the specific credentialing, clearance, and timeline requirements that general job boards do not address.

### Navigating the Government Contract Hiring Process

The hiring process for contract athletic trainer positions typically follows this sequence:

1. **Application submission** through the contractor's career portal or through a recruiter
2. **Initial screening** for BOC certification, education, and relevant experience
3. **Interview** with the contractor's program manager and potentially the on-site lead
4. **Conditional offer** pending background investigation and credentialing
5. **Background investigation and security clearance processing** (several weeks to months)
6. **Credentialing verification** including state licensure and BOC status
7. **Start date and onboarding** at the installation

The entire process from application to start date can take 6-12 weeks, sometimes longer if clearance processing is delayed. Beginning the process well before your desired start date is strongly recommended.

### Resume Tips for Military Athletic Trainer Applications

When applying for athletic trainer jobs at Joint Base Lewis-McChord, tailor your resume to emphasize:

- Specific clinical skills relevant to musculoskeletal injury management
- Experience with active or tactical populations
- Familiarity with movement screening tools and evidence-based rehabilitation protocols
- Data tracking and outcomes documentation experience
- Ability to work independently and within interdisciplinary teams
- Any prior military installation or government contract experience

Quantify your impact wherever possible. "Managed rehabilitation caseload of 45+ athletes" communicates more than "provided rehabilitation services."
