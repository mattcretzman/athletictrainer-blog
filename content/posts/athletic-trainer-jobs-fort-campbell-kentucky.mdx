## At a Glance: Athletic Trainer Jobs at Fort Campbell

Athletic trainers at Fort Campbell, Kentucky work as part of the Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) program, supporting approximately 26,000 soldiers of the 101st Airborne Division (Air Assault). Positions are typically filled through government contractors, require BOC certification and state licensure, and involve injury prevention, movement screening, acute care, and return-to-duty rehabilitation for tactical athletes. With multiple Brigade Combat Teams on the installation, Fort Campbell often has several concurrent athletic trainer openings at any given time.

If you are a certified athletic trainer considering a move into military healthcare, Fort Campbell represents one of the most established and operationally significant postings in the Department of Defense system. This guide covers everything you need to know — from the scope of the role and required qualifications to salary expectations, the hiring process, and what daily life looks like on and around the installation.

## Why Fort Campbell Is a Top Destination for Athletic Trainer Jobs

### Overview of Fort Campbell: Mission, Size, and Military Significance

Fort Campbell straddles the Kentucky-Tennessee border, approximately 60 miles northwest of Nashville. The installation encompasses roughly 105,000 acres and serves as one of the Army's premier power projection platforms, meaning its units are trained and equipped to deploy rapidly anywhere in the world.

The post is home to approximately 26,000 active-duty soldiers, along with tens of thousands of family members and civilian employees. Its medical infrastructure is anchored by Blanchfield Army Community Hospital (BACH), a full-service medical center that also serves as the credentialing authority for healthcare providers — including athletic trainers — working on the installation.

For athletic trainers, the sheer concentration of physically active service members creates a sustained, high-volume demand for musculoskeletal injury prevention and performance optimization services.

### The 101st Airborne Division and the Demand for Athletic Trainers

The 101st Airborne Division (Air Assault) is the Army's only air assault division and one of its most frequently deployed units. The physical demands placed on these soldiers are significant: air assault operations involve fast-roping from helicopters, long foot movements under heavy loads, and sustained operations in austere environments.

This operational tempo translates directly into a need for athletic trainers who understand tactical athlete performance. Soldiers in the 101st experience high rates of musculoskeletal injuries — particularly to the lower extremities, lumbar spine, and shoulders — making injury prevention and early intervention critical to unit readiness.

Fort Campbell also hosts the 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment (Night Stalkers), an elite unit whose personnel may have access to specialized performance support, potentially opening additional athletic trainer roles for those with experience supporting special operations populations.

### How the H2F Program Created a Surge in Military AT Positions

The Army's Holistic Health and Fitness (H2F) system is the primary framework through which athletic trainers are embedded at installations like Fort Campbell. Mandated for implementation across all Brigade Combat Teams by 2030, H2F represents the most significant expansion of [athletic trainer jobs on military bases](/blog/athletic-trainer-jobs-military-bases) in the program's history.

Each H2F team at the brigade level typically includes one to two certified athletic trainers working alongside strength and conditioning coaches, physical therapists, registered dietitians, occupational therapists, and cognitive performance specialists. Fort Campbell, with its multiple Brigade Combat Teams under the 101st Airborne, supports several of these interdisciplinary teams simultaneously.

This means Fort Campbell is not a single-position installation. At any given time, multiple athletic trainer jobs at Fort Campbell may be open across different brigades, making it one of the more reliable locations for finding available roles.

## Athletic Trainer Roles and Responsibilities at Fort Campbell

### What Does a Typical Day Look Like for an AT on a Military Installation?

Your day at Fort Campbell will look different from a traditional sports medicine clinic or collegiate athletic training room. A typical schedule begins early — often by 0600 — because that is when most units conduct physical training (PT).

During PT, you may be on the field or at the Tactical Athlete Performance Center (TAPC) providing:

- **Pre-activity movement screens** using tools like the Functional Movement Screen (FMS) or similar protocols
- **Acute injury assessment and management** for sprains, strains, and overuse injuries sustained during training
- **Taping, bracing, and protective equipment fitting** for soldiers returning to activity
- **Education and corrective exercise instruction** for individuals with identified movement deficiencies

After the morning PT window, your schedule may shift to scheduled appointments for injury follow-ups, rehabilitation progressions, and return-to-duty evaluations. Afternoons often include documentation in the military's electronic health record system, coordination with other H2F team members, and planning for upcoming training events — such as ruck marches, field training exercises, or air assault qualification courses.

The rhythm of the work follows the unit's training calendar, not a traditional clinical schedule. This means your caseload and intensity will fluctuate with the operational cycle.

### Working Within the H2F Interdisciplinary Team

One of the defining features of [H2F athletic trainer positions](/blog/h2f-athletic-trainer-positions) is the interdisciplinary team model. You will not work in isolation. Your closest collaborators on a daily basis typically include:

- **Strength and Conditioning Coaches (CSCS):** Coordinating on physical readiness training and load management
- **Physical Therapists:** Collaborating on rehabilitation protocols and referral pathways
- **Registered Dietitians:** Addressing nutritional factors that influence injury recovery and performance
- **Cognitive Performance Specialists:** Supporting the mental readiness component of soldier performance
- **Unit Leadership (Company Commanders, First Sergeants):** Communicating soldier readiness status and training modifications

This team-based model often provides a level of professional collaboration that surpasses what many athletic trainers experience in civilian settings. Your clinical judgment carries weight in readiness decisions, and the team structure encourages cross-disciplinary problem solving.

### Scope of Practice: How Military AT Roles Differ from Civilian Settings

If you are transitioning from a civilian sports medicine environment, there are several meaningful differences to anticipate at Fort Campbell.

**Broader patient demographic.** Your patient population spans a wider age and fitness range than a typical college or professional sports team. You will work with 18-year-old privates and 45-year-old senior noncommissioned officers, each with distinct injury profiles and performance goals.

**Emphasis on readiness, not return-to-play.** The framework shifts from "return to sport" to "return to duty." This involves understanding occupational physical demands — how much weight a soldier carries, the distance and terrain of planned movements, whether they need to qualify on a weapon — and tailoring your care accordingly.

**Greater autonomy in many cases.** Depending on the brigade and the supervising physician's standing orders, you may have significant latitude to evaluate, treat, and manage musculoskeletal conditions without a same-day physician referral. This scope of practice often exceeds what athletic trainers experience in states with restrictive practice acts.

**Documentation and military systems.** You will document in the Armed Forces Health Longitudinal Technology Application (AHLTA) or its successor system, MHS GENESIS. Familiarity with military health record systems is not required at hire but will be part of your onboarding.

### Key Units and Facilities Where ATs Are Embedded

Athletic trainers at Fort Campbell are primarily embedded at the brigade level within the 101st Airborne Division's Brigade Combat Teams. Current and potential embedding locations include:

- **1st, 2nd, and 3rd Brigade Combat Teams (BCTs)** — each with its own H2F team and TAPC
- **101st Combat Aviation Brigade** — supporting aviation soldiers with unique occupational demands
- **101st Sustainment Brigade** — supporting logistics and support soldiers
- **Blanchfield Army Community Hospital** — potential roles within orthopedic or sports medicine clinics
- **Soldier Recovery Unit** — supporting wounded, ill, or injured soldiers through rehabilitation and transition

The variety of units means that your experience at Fort Campbell can differ meaningfully depending on your assignment. An athletic trainer embedded with an infantry BCT will have a different daily rhythm than one supporting an aviation brigade.

## Qualifications and Requirements for Fort Campbell AT Positions

### BOC Certification and State Licensure Requirements (Kentucky and Tennessee)

All athletic trainer positions at Fort Campbell require current certification from the Board of Certification (BOC). This is non-negotiable and must remain active throughout your employment.

The licensure question is more nuanced because Fort Campbell straddles two states. Kentucky and Tennessee each have their own athletic training licensure boards and requirements. Most contract positions require you to hold a valid license in the state where your primary practice site is located. In practice, many athletic trainers at Fort Campbell obtain licensure in both states to ensure compliance regardless of where their duties take them.

It is worth noting that federal installations operate under federal authority, and some contracts may reference the federal supremacy clause regarding licensure. However, the standard expectation from most government contractors and credentialing authorities is that you hold valid state licensure. Clarify the specific requirement with the hiring contractor before assuming federal preemption applies.

For more detail on [BOC certification requirements for military AT jobs](/blog/boc-certification-requirements-military-at-jobs), PSI maintains a dedicated resource page.

### Security Clearance and Background Check Process

Athletic trainer positions at Fort Campbell typically require a National Agency Check with Inquiries (NACI) or a Tier 1 background investigation. This is the baseline investigation for access to a federal installation and is less extensive than a Secret or Top Secret clearance.

The NACI process involves:

- Submission of an SF-86 or equivalent background questionnaire
- Verification of employment history, education, and references
- A criminal background check and credit review
- Fingerprinting

Processing times vary, but you should plan for four to eight weeks from submission to adjudication. Some contractors can provide interim access while your investigation is pending, but this depends on the contract and the installation's security office.

If you are being considered for a role supporting the 160th Special Operations Aviation Regiment, a higher-level clearance may be required. Your recruiter will communicate this during the application process.

### Credentialing Through Blanchfield Army Community Hospital

As a healthcare provider on a military installation, you must be credentialed through the local medical treatment facility — in this case, Blanchfield Army Community Hospital. This process is separate from your background check and involves:

- Verification of your BOC certification and state license
- Review of your education and clinical training records
- Verification of malpractice history and any disciplinary actions
- Issuance of clinical privileges defining your authorized scope of practice

The credentialing process can take several weeks, and your contractor will typically guide you through the required documentation. Having all your credentials organized, current, and easily accessible before you begin the process will help avoid delays.

### Preferred Experience and Additional Certifications

While the minimum qualification is BOC certification and state licensure, competitive candidates for Fort Campbell positions often bring:

- **Two or more years of clinical experience**, particularly with active or athletic populations
- **Experience with musculoskeletal screening protocols** such as FMS, SFMA, or Y-Balance Test
- **Emergency cardiac care certification** (BLS at minimum; ACLS is a differentiator)
- **Certified Strength and Conditioning Specialist (CSCS)** or Performance Enhancement Specialist credential
- **Familiarity with military culture**, even if you have never worked on an installation
- **Manual therapy skills** such as dry needling (if within state scope of practice), instrument-assisted soft tissue mobilization, or joint mobilization techniques

<Callout type="tip">
If you do not yet have military experience, consider volunteering at a Veterans Affairs clinic or reaching out to local National Guard units for observation opportunities. Even modest familiarity with military rank structure, terminology, and culture can make a meaningful difference during interviews and in your first weeks on the job.
</Callout>

## How to Apply: Navigating the Government Contract Hiring Process

### Understanding Government Contractors and How AT Jobs Are Awarded

Athletic trainer positions at Fort Campbell are not direct federal hires. They are contract positions, meaning the Department of Defense awards multi-year contracts to private companies, and those companies hire and manage the clinical staff.

The contract vehicle is typically awarded through a competitive bidding process managed by Army Medical Command (MEDCOM) or through specific program offices responsible for H2F implementation. Contracts may change hands when they are rebid, which can affect employment continuity — though new contractors often retain incumbent staff who are performing well.

Understanding this structure is important because it means you will apply through the contracting company, not through the Army directly. Your employer is the contractor, and your benefits, salary, and HR processes are managed by that company.

### Applying Through PSI and Other Military Healthcare Recruiters

PSI is among the government contractors that supports the placement of athletic trainers in H2F and related programs at military installations, including Fort Campbell. The application process through PSI typically follows these steps:

1. **Identify open positions** on the [PSI military healthcare careers](/careers) portal or through a PSI recruiter
2. **Submit your application** including your resume, BOC certification, state license, and any additional credentials
3. **Initial screening call** with a PSI recruiter to discuss the role, your background, and your interest
4. **Formal interview** which may include a panel with the contractor's clinical leadership and, in some cases, military stakeholders
5. **Conditional offer** contingent on successful completion of background check and credentialing
6. **Onboarding and credentialing** including security investigation submission and BACH credentialing

The timeline from application to start date is typically 8 to 16 weeks, depending on the speed of background check processing and credentialing. Plan accordingly, especially if you need to relocate.

### What to Expect During the Interview and Onboarding Timeline

Interviews for Fort Campbell athletic trainer positions often focus on:

- Your clinical decision-making process for common musculoskeletal presentations
- Experience with injury prevention programming at scale (working with large groups)
- Ability to function as part of an interdisciplinary team
- Understanding of military culture and the unique demands of tactical athletes
- Flexibility and adaptability in a non-traditional clinical environment

Be prepared to discuss specific clinical scenarios. Interviewers may ask how you would manage an acute ankle injury during a field training exercise with limited equipment, or how you would design an injury prevention program for a battalion preparing for a 12-mile ruck march.

### Tips for Standing Out as a Candidate

<Callout type="tip">
When applying for athletic trainer jobs at Fort Campbell, tailor your resume to highlight experience that translates to the military setting. Quantify your impact where possible — injury reduction rates, return-to-activity timelines, volume of patient encounters. Hiring managers and military stakeholders respond to data-driven outcomes, not just descriptions of duties.
</Callout>

Additional ways to differentiate yourself:

- **Research the 101st Airborne Division's mission and structure.** Demonstrating knowledge of the unit during your interview shows genuine interest and preparation.
- **Highlight interdisciplinary collaboration experience.** H2F values team-based care, so any background working alongside physical therapists, dietitians, or performance coaches is relevant.
- **Be transparent about your learning curve.** If you lack military experience, acknowledge it honestly while describing your plan to learn quickly. Authenticity is valued.

## Salary, Benefits, and Compensation at Fort Campbell

### Typical Salary Ranges for Contract Athletic Trainers

Contract athletic trainer positions at Fort Campbell offer competitive salary packages that vary by location and contract terms.

Factors that may push compensation toward the higher end of this range include:

- Prior military installation experience
- Additional certifications (CSCS, manual therapy credentials)
- Willingness to support field exercises or temporary duty assignments

Some contracts also offer relocation assistance or signing incentives, particularly when positions have been difficult to fill. Ask your recruiter specifically about these benefits during the screening process.

### Benefits Packages: Health Insurance, PTO, and Retirement

As a contract employee, your benefits come from the contracting company, not from the federal government. Typical benefits packages include:

- **Health, dental, and vision insurance** — often with multiple tier options
- **Paid time off** — usually 10 to 15 days annually, plus federal holidays
- **401(k) or retirement plan** — some contractors offer matching contributions
- **Continuing education allowance** — varies by contractor, but many provide annual allowances for CEUs and professional development
- **Life insurance and disability coverage**

Benefits vary significantly between contractors, so compare offers carefully. The salary number alone does not tell the full story.

### How Fort Campbell Compensation Compares to Other Installations

Fort Campbell's compensation is competitive with other Army installations in similar cost-of-living areas. Compared to installations in high-cost metropolitan areas (such as Joint Base Lewis-McChord near Seattle or Fort Belvoir near Washington, D.C.), the base salary may appear lower. However, the [cost of living near Fort Campbell](#cost-of-living-breakdown-and-housing-recommendations) is 10 to 15 percent below the national average, which means your effective purchasing power is often equivalent or superior.

For a broader comparison across installations, PSI maintains a resource on [certified athletic trainer salary in military settings](/blog/certified-athletic-trainer-salary-military).

## Installation Guide: Living and Working at Fort Campbell

### Getting On-Base: Installation Access and CAC Card Process

As a contractor providing healthcare services on Fort Campbell, you will be issued a Common Access Card (CAC), which serves as your identification and access credential. The CAC enables you to:

- Enter the installation through access control points
- Access military healthcare IT systems
- Enter secured buildings and clinical facilities

Your CAC is typically issued during the onboarding process after your background investigation has been favorably adjudicated. Until your CAC is ready, you may receive a temporary visitor pass that requires escort or is limited to certain access points. Your contractor's on-site manager will coordinate this process.

Plan to arrive at the Visitor Control Center with two forms of government-issued identification, your employment authorization letter, and patience — processing can take several hours during peak times.

### On-Post Amenities, Fitness Facilities, and Daily Life

Fort Campbell is a small city unto itself. On-post amenities available to contractors with CAC access typically include:

- **Post Exchange (PX) and Commissary** — retail shopping and grocery options (commissary access for contractors may be limited; verify your eligibility)
- **Multiple fitness centers and gymnasiums** — relevant both for your own wellness and for understanding the training environments your soldiers use
- **Dining facilities and food courts**
- **Post library, outdoor recreation areas, and MWR (Morale, Welfare, and Recreation) programs**

The Tactical Athlete Performance Centers (TAPCs) where you will spend much of your working day are typically well-equipped with treatment tables, rehabilitation equipment, and space for movement screening and corrective exercise programming.

### Clarksville, TN and Hopkinsville, KY: Off-Post Living Options

Most contract athletic trainers at Fort Campbell live off-post. The two primary communities are:

**Clarksville, Tennessee** (population approximately 170,000) is the more popular choice. Located immediately south of Fort Campbell, Clarksville offers a growing restaurant and retail scene, a regional medical center, multiple parks and greenways, and a vibrant community influenced by its proximity to both Nashville and the military installation. The commute from most Clarksville neighborhoods to the installation gates is 10 to 25 minutes.

**Hopkinsville, Kentucky** (population approximately 30,000) is smaller and more rural, located to the north of the installation. Housing costs tend to be lower than Clarksville, but amenities and dining options are more limited. The commute is similar in duration.

### Cost of Living Breakdown and Housing Recommendations

The Clarksville-Hopkinsville area offers a cost of living approximately 10 to 15 percent below the national average. Here is a general breakdown:

| Category | Approximate Monthly Cost |
|---|---|
| One-bedroom apartment (Clarksville) | $850 - $1,100 |
| Two-bedroom apartment (Clarksville) | $1,000 - $1,400 |
| Mortgage (median home, Clarksville) | $1,200 - $1,600 |
| Groceries | At or slightly below national average |
| Utilities | $150 - $200 |
| Gas/transportation | Below national average |

Tennessee has no state income tax on wages, which is a meaningful financial advantage if you reside in Clarksville. Kentucky does levy a state income tax, so your state of residence — not just your work location — affects your take-home pay.

### Family Life, Schools, and Community Near Fort Campbell

For athletic trainers relocating with families, the Clarksville-Montgomery County School System serves the area south of the installation and has earned generally positive reviews. The district operates several schools both on-post (Department of Defense Education Activity schools for military dependents) and off-post. Contractor family members typically attend off-post public schools.

Clarksville's proximity to Nashville (about an hour's drive) provides access to professional sports, concerts, museums, and a major airport. The surrounding area offers outdoor recreation including Land Between the Lakes National Recreation Area, Dunbar Cave State Park, and the Cumberland River greenway system.

The community around Fort Campbell is deeply connected to the military, which creates a welcoming environment for those supporting the installation's mission — even as civilian contractors.

## Career Growth and Professional Development Opportunities

### Continuing Education and CEU Opportunities on Military Installations

Working at Fort Campbell provides access to professional development opportunities that may not be available in civilian settings. These include:

- **In-service training** through the H2F program on tactical athlete performance, military-specific injury patterns, and evidence-based rehabilitation protocols
- **Collaboration with military medical professionals** including orthopedic surgeons, sports medicine physicians, and physical therapists at Blanchfield Army Community Hospital
- **Contractor-funded CEU allowances** for attending conferences such as the NATA Annual Meeting, TSAC Annual Training, or military-specific symposia
- **Access to research and data** on musculoskeletal injury trends across large soldier populations

### Advancing Within the H2F System or Transitioning to Other Bases

Fort Campbell can serve as a launching point for a long-term career in military sports medicine. Advancement pathways include:

- **Lead Athletic Trainer or Clinical Coordinator** roles within a brigade or across the installation
- **Transfer to other installations** where your contractor holds additional H2F contracts
- **Transition to special operations support** roles, such as those with the 160th SOAR or at other USASOC installations
- **Movement into program management** for contractors who need experienced clinicians in leadership positions

The H2F program's continued expansion means that experienced military athletic trainers will be increasingly sought after as the Army scales the program to all Brigade Combat Teams. Establishing your career at an installation like Fort Campbell positions you as a subject matter expert in a growing field.

### Building a Long-Term Career in Military Sports Medicine

The military athletic training career path offers a stability and mission clarity that many athletic trainers find professionally fulfilling. The tactical athlete population is motivated, the injuries are clinically interesting, and the interdisciplinary team model supports professional growth.

PSI encourages athletic trainers to view Fort Campbell not as a temporary assignment but as a potential cornerstone of a meaningful career in military healthcare.
